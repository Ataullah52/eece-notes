Unit 1 — Circuit Variables and Elements

Sources: Sadiku Ch.1 · Theraja Ch.11 · past papers 2021–2025. ★★★ = asked almost every year.

1.1 Charge, current, voltage

Charge \(q\): basic electrical quantity, unit coulomb (C). \(e=1.602\times10^{-19}\) C, so 1 C ≈ \(6.24\times10^{18}\) electrons.
Current: rate of flow of charge, \(i=\dfrac{dq}{dt}\), \(q=\int i\,dt\). Unit ampere (1 A = 1 C/s). Direction = flow of positive charge (opposite to electrons).
Voltage: energy per unit charge between two points, \(v=\dfrac{dw}{dq}\). 1 V = 1 J/C. The + terminal is at higher potential.

1.2 Power, energy and sign convention

Power \(p=\dfrac{dw}{dt}=vi\) (watt). Energy \(w=\int p\,dt\) (joule); 1 kWh = \(3.6\times10^{6}\) J; constant power: \(E=Pt\).
Passive sign convention. Current enters the + terminal → \(p=+vi\) is absorbed. Current leaves the + terminal → \(p=-vi\): the element supplies. In any circuit \(\sum p=0\). Use this to check every answer.

1.5 Non-sinusoidal waves: average and RMS

Average value \(X_{avg}=\dfrac1T\int_0^T x\,dt\) (area ÷ period; for a symmetric wave use the half cycle). RMS (effective) value \(X_{rms}=\sqrt{\dfrac1T\int_0^T x^2\,dt}\): the DC value giving the same heating. Form factor \(K_f=X_{rms}/X_{avg}\). Peak factor \(K_p=X_m/X_{rms}\).
Why important: RMS gives the effective value (\(P=I^2R\)), so supplies and meters are rated in RMS (230 V mains). Average is what a DC meter reads and what rectifiers and chargers deliver. Form and peak factor describe wave shape and insulation needs.

Method for any piecewise wave: 1) find T · 2) write each segment (line: \(y=mt+c\)) · 3) integrate \(x\) or \(x^2\) per segment · 4) add, divide by T · 5) RMS: take the square root · 6) power in R: \(P=I_{rms}^2R\).
Why \(V_{rms}=V_m/\sqrt2\) (2021 Q5b). For \(v=V_m\cos\omega t\), use \(\cos^2\theta=\tfrac12(1+\cos2\theta)\): \[V_{rms}^2=\frac{V_m^2}{T}\int_0^T\tfrac12(1+\cos2\omega t)\,dt=\frac{V_m^2}{T}\cdot\frac T2=\frac{V_m^2}{2}\] The \(\cos2\omega t\) term integrates to zero over a full cycle, so \(V_{rms}=0.707V_m\).

1.6 Solved problems from past papers

P1 · 2025 Q1(c): RMS of the current wave and average power in a 2 Ω resistor.

T = 4 s. 0<t<2: \(i=5t\). 2<t<4: \(i=-10\) A.

\[I_{rms}^2=\frac14\Big[\int_0^2(5t)^2dt+\int_2^4(-10)^2dt\Big]=\frac14\Big[25\cdot\frac83+200\Big]=66.67\]

Irms = 8.165 A, P = Irms²R = 133.3 W

P2 · 2025 Q7(c): average and effective value of the square wave (20 V for 0.1 s, then 0 V for 0.2 s).

T = 0.3 s. \(V_{avg}=\dfrac{20(0.1)}{0.3}=6.67\) V. \(V_{rms}^2=\dfrac{20^2(0.1)}{0.3}=133.3\).

Vavg = 6.67 V, Vrms = 11.55 V

P3 · 2024 Q1(a) (2022 Q1a same type): \(i=10\cos60\pi t\) A at t = 4 ms; power for (a) \(v=3i\), (b) \(v=3\,di/dt\).

Angle \(=60\pi(0.004)=43.2^\circ\), \(i=10\cos43.2^\circ=7.290\) A.

(a) \(v=3i=21.87\) V, \(p=3i^2=159.4\) W (absorbed).

(b) \(di/dt=-600\pi\sin43.2^\circ=-1290.3\) A/s, \(v=-3871\) V, \(p=vi=-28.2\) kW: the inductor supplies power at that instant.

(a) 159.4 W absorbed · (b) −28.2 kW (supplied)

p₁ = −40 W, p₂ = 16 W, p₃ = 9 W, p₄ = 15 W

1.8 Quick revision

\(i=\dfrac{dq}{dt}\) · \(v=\dfrac{dw}{dq}\) · \(p=vi\) · \(w=\int p\,dt\) · \(\sum p=0\) · \(W_L=\tfrac12Li^2\) · \(W_C=\tfrac12Cv^2=\dfrac{Q^2}{2C}\) · sine: 0.707, 0.637, 1.11, 1.414
  • Full-cycle average of a symmetric wave is 0: quote the half-cycle value.
  • For RMS, square first, then average, then root.
  • Check the + terminal and the arrow for the power sign, then confirm \(\sum p=0\).
  • The period of a pulse train includes the zero interval.

Three Standard Cases for Computing Thevenin Resistance \((R_{T}h)\)

P1 · Class Note · Memorize & Quick Recall · Class Note · Lecture 10 (Three Cases for Finding R_Th)

How to find \(R_{T}h\) for circuits with (Case 1) only independent sources, (Case 2) both independent & dependent sources, and (Case 3) only dependent sources.

Choosing the right method to compute \(R_{T}h\) depends strictly on whether dependent sources exist inside the linear two-terminal network.

Table 4.1 — Complete Procedure for Finding Thevenin Equivalent \((V_{T}h, R_{T}h)\)
Circuit Case\(V_{T}h\) Calculation\(R_{T}h\) Calculation Procedure
Case 1: Independent Sources OnlyFind open-circuit voltage \(v_{o}c\) across terminals \(a-b\)Turn off all independent sources (Voltage source \(\to \) Short, Current source \(\to \) Open) and find \(R_{eq}\).
Case 2: Both Independent & Dependent SourcesFind open-circuit voltage \(v_{o}c\) across terminals \(a-b\)Keep dependent sources active! Either (a) apply test source \(v_{o} = 1 V\) after deactivating independent sources \((R_{T}h = \frac{v_{o}}{i_{o}})\), or (b) find \(i_{s}c\) and compute \(R_{T}h = \frac{v_{o}c}{i_{s}c}\).
Case 3: Dependent Sources Only (No Independent Sources)\(V_{T}h = 0 V\) identically (dead active network)Apply external test voltage \(v_{o} = 1 V\) (or \(i_{o} = 1 A\)) at terminals \(a-b\) and calculate \(R_{T}h = \frac{v_{o}}{i_{o}}\).

Class tip: Never turn off a dependent source when calculating \(R_{T}h\) or using Superposition! Also remember that power cannot be found by summing individual Superposition powers \((p \neq p_{1} + p_{2})\) because \(p = i^{2}R\) is nonlinear.

Maximum Power Transfer Theorem & Proof \((\frac{dP_{L}}{dR_{L}} = 0)\)

P2 · Previous Year Question · Definition & Law · Previous Year Question · Final Exam 2023 Q4(a), 2022 Q4(a), 2020 Q4(b) · Asked in: 2023, 2022, 2020 (6 Marks)

A resistive load \(R_{L}\) receives maximum power from a linear DC network when \(R_{L} = R_{T}h\), yielding \(P_{\max} = \frac{V_{T}h^{2}}{4R_{T}h}\) at 50% efficiency.

Replacing any linear circuit connected to a variable load resistor \(R_{L}\) with its Thevenin equivalent (\(V_{T}h\) in series with \(R_{T}h\)), the load current is \(I_{L} = \frac{V_{T}h}{R_{T}h + R_{L}}\) and the load power is \(P_{L} = I_{L}^{2} \cdot R_{L}\).

Differentiating \(P_{L}\) with respect to \(R_{L}\) using the quotient rule and setting \(\frac{dP_{L}}{dR_{L}} = 0\) proves that maximum power is transferred when the load resistance matches the internal Thevenin resistance \((R_{L} = R_{T}h)\).

Load Power as Function of \(R_{L}\) (Watts (W))
\(P_{L} = [\frac{V_{Th}}{R_{Th} + R_{L}}]^{2} \cdot R_{L}\)
Zero-Derivative Optimality Condition (Ohms \((\Omega )\))
\(\frac{dP_{L}}{dR_{L}} = V_{Th}^{2} \cdot \frac{(R_{Th} + R_{L})^{2} - 2R_{L}(R_{Th} + R_{L})}{(R_{Th} + R_{L})^{4}} = 0 \implies R_{L} = R_{Th}\)
Maximum Transferred Power Formula (Watts (W) at \(\eta = 50\%\) efficiency)
\(P_{\max} = \frac{V_{Th}^{2}}{4 \cdot R_{Th}}\)

Millman’s Theorem, Substitution Theorem & Reciprocity Theorem

P3 · Professor-Followed Book · Core Theory & Concept · Professor-Followed Book · B. L. Theraja Vol. I & Boylestad Ch. 9

Millman’s Theorem combines \(N\) parallel voltage branches into a single equivalent source \(V_{eq} = \frac{\sum V_{k} G_{k}}{\sum G_{k}}\).

Millman’s Theorem simplifies parallel branches of voltage sources in series with resistors by converting each branch to its Norton current equivalent, summing the parallel conductances \(G_{k} = \frac{1}{R_{k}}\), and converting back to a single Thevenin source.

The Reciprocity Theorem states that in any linear bilateral single-source network, the ratio of excitation voltage in loop A to the response current in loop \(B\) remains invariant when the positions of the voltage source and ammeter are interchanged.

Millman’s Equivalent Voltage & Resistance (\(G_{k} = \frac{1}{R_{k}}\) (Siemens))
\(V_{eq} = \frac{\sum _{k=1}^{N} V_{k} \cdot G_{k}}{\sum _{k=1}^{N} G_{k}}, R_{eq} = \frac{1}{\sum _{k=1}^{N} G_{k}}\)

Worked Millman’s Theorem Problem for Three Parallel Battery Branches

P4 · Syllabus Book · Worked Numerical Example · Syllabus Book · Robert L. Boylestad & R. P. Ward (Ch. 9)

Find the terminal voltage \(V_{L}\) across \(a 10 \Omega \) load fed by three parallel branches: \((12 V, 2 \Omega ), (6 V, 3 \Omega )\), and \((24 V, 6 \Omega )\).

Problem / Theorem Question

Three parallel DC voltage branches — Branch 1 (12 V in series with \(2 \Omega \)), Branch 2 (6 V in series with \(3 \Omega \)), and Branch 3 (24 V in series with \(6 \Omega ) —\) supply a common load resistor \(R_{L} = 9 \Omega \). Find \(V_{eq}, R_{eq}\), and load current \(I_{L}\) using Millman’s Theorem.

Step-by-step solution
Step 1: Compute \(\sum V_{k} G_{k}\) and \(\sum G_{k}\)

With \(G_{1} = \frac{1}{2} S, G_{2} = \frac{1}{3} S, G_{3} = \frac{1}{6} S\):

\(\sum G_{k} = \frac{1}{2} + \frac{1}{3} + \frac{1}{6}\)

\(= 1.0 S\)

\(\implies R_{eq} = 1.0 \Omega \)

\(\sum V_{k} G_{k} = 12(\frac{1}{2}) + 6(\frac{1}{3}) + 24(\frac{1}{6})\)

\(= 6 + 2 + 4\)

\(= 12 A\)

∴ \(V_{eq} = 12 V, R_{eq} = 1.0 \Omega , I_{L} = \frac{12}{1 + 9} = 1.2 A\)

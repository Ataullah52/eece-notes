Passive Sign Convention (PSC) & Power Direction Criterion

★★★★★ 01. Core Theory & Concept · Class Note · Lecture 01 & 02 (Professor Board Emphasis)

  • Whenever reference current \(i\) enters the positive (+) voltage terminal, use \(p = +vi\).
  • If current enters the negative (−) terminal, use \(p = -vi\).
  • In circuit analysis, assigning power as absorbed or supplied requires inspecting the relative orientation of the voltage polarity (+ / −) and the current arrow before substituting signed numbers.
  • Under the Passive Sign Convention (PSC), if the current arrow enters the positive (+) terminal of an element, calculate instantaneous power as \(p = +v \cdot i\).
  • Conversely, if the current arrow enters the negative (−) terminal (leaves the positive terminal), negate the expression to \(p = -v \cdot i\).
  • Result \(p > 0 W\): The element is absorbing (consuming) electric power — behaves as a passive load or resistor.
  • Result \(p < 0 W\): The element is supplying (delivering) electric power to the circuit — behaves as an active source.
  • Conservation of Power Check: In any isolated circuit at any instant \(t\), the algebraic sum of power across all elements is strictly zero \((\sum p = 0)\).
Current Entering + Terminal (Watts \((W = \frac{J}{s})\))
\(p(t) = +v(t) \cdot i(t)\)
  • Standard Passive Sign Convention orientation
Current Leaving + Terminal (Watts (W))
\(p(t) = -v(t) \cdot i(t)\)
  • Active generator orientation
Total Circuit Power Balance (Watts (W))
\(\sum p_{a}bsorbed = \sum p_{s}upplied <=> \sum p_{k} = 0\)
  • Mandatory verification check in exam problems

Class tip: In semester exams, always write two explicit steps: (1) identify whether current enters the + or − terminal to fix the formula sign, and (2) substitute the numerical signs of \(v\) and \(i\) inside parentheses.

Fundamental Definitions: Electric Charge, Current, Voltage & Energy

★★★★★ 02. Definition & Law · Previous Year Question · Final Exam 2023 Q1(a), 2021 Q1(a), 2019 Q1(b) · Asked in: 2023, 2021, 2019 (4 Marks)

  • Electric charge \(q\) is the fundamental electrical property of matter in Coulombs (C).
  • Electric current \(i = \frac{dq}{dt}\) is the time rate of change of charge in Amperes (A).
  • Voltage \(v_{a}b = \frac{dw}{dq}\) is the work required per unit charge in Volts (V).
  • Electric Charge (q) is the fundamental electrical property of atomic matter, measured in Coulombs (C).
  • One electron carries a negative charge of \(-1.602 \times 10^{-19} C\), meaning 1 C equals the charge of \(6.24 \times 10^{18}\) electrons.
  • Electric Current (i) is the time rate of change of charge passing through a specified cross-sectional area, measured in Amperes (A).
  • Voltage or Potential Difference \((v_{a}b)\) between node a and node \(b\) is the energy (work) required to move a unit positive charge (+1 C) from terminal b (−) to terminal a (+).
Instantaneous Current (\(1 A = 1 \frac{C}{s}\))
\(i(t) = \frac{dq(t)}{dt}\)
Net Charge Transferred (Coulombs (C))
\(q(t) = \int [t0\) to \(t] i(\tau ) d\tau + q(t0\))
Voltage (Potential Difference) (\(1 V = 1 \frac{J}{C} = 1 \frac{N\cdot m}{C}\))
\(v_{ab} = \frac{dw}{dq}\)
Instantaneous Power via Chain Rule (\(1 W = 1 \frac{J}{s}\))
\(p = \frac{dw}{dt} = (\frac{dw}{dq}) \cdot (\frac{dq}{dt}) = v \cdot i\)

Time-Varying Charge Waveform & Instantaneous Power Delivered

★★★★★ 04. Worked Numerical Example · Class Note · Lecture 03 Worked Problem (Professor Board Example)

  • Given \(q(t) = 5t\cdot \sin(4\pi t) mC\) and \(v(t) = 2\cdot \cos(4\pi t) V\), determine \(i(t)\) and \(p(t)\) at \(t = 0.25 s\).
Problem / Theorem Question
  • The total charge entering the positive terminal of a circuit element is given by \(q(t) = 5t \cdot \sin(4\pi t) mC\), while the voltage across the element (with + at the entry terminal) is \(v(t) = 2 \cdot \cos(4\pi t) V\). Calculate the current \(i(t)\) and the power absorbed at \(t = 0.25 s\).

Given parameters

  • \(q(t) = 5t \cdot \sin(4\pi t) mC\)
  • \(v(t) = 2 \cdot \cos(4\pi t) V\)
  • Evaluation time \(t = 0.25 s\) (Note: \(4\pi t\) is in radians)
Step-by-step solution
Step 1: Differentiate charge \(q(t)\) using the product rule

Since current enters the positive terminal, \(i(t) = \frac{dq}{dt}\):

\(i(t) = \frac{d}{dt} [5t \cdot \sin(4\pi t)]\)

\(= 5 \cdot \sin(4\pi t) + 20\pi t \cdot \cos(4\pi t) mA\)

Step 2: Evaluate \(i(t)\) and \(v(t)\) at \(t = 0.25 s\)

Substitute \(t = 0.25 s\) so that angle \(4\pi (0.25) = \pi \) rad (where \(\sin(\pi ) = 0\) and \(\cos(\pi ) = -1\)):

\(i(0.25) = 5\cdot \sin(\pi ) + 20\pi (0.25)\cdot \cos(\pi )\)

\(= 0 + 5\pi (-1)\)

\(= -15.708 mA\)

\(v(0.25) = 2 \cdot \cos(\pi )\)

\(= -2.0 V\)

Step 3: Compute power p(0.25) using Passive Sign Convention

Because current enters the positive terminal, \(p = +v \cdot i\):

\(p(0.25) = (-2.0 V) \times (-15.708 mA)\)

\(= +31.42 mW\) (Absorbing)

∴ \(i(0.25 s) = -15.71 mA, p(0.25 s) = +31.42 mW\) (Absorbed)

Conservation of Power & Unknown Dependent Source Current in a 5-Element Network

★★★★ 05. Previous Year Question (PYQ) · Previous Year Question · Semester Final 2023 Q1(c) & 2020 Q1(b) · Asked in: 2023, 2020 (7 Marks)

  • Calculate the power of each element in a 5-branch network and verify \(\sum p = 0\) to find unknown current \(i_{x}\).
Problem / Theorem Question
  • A closed 5-element DC network has measured branch powers: \(p1 = -205 W\) (Independent 25 V Source), \(p2 = +60 W\) (Resistor \(R1), p3 = +45 W\) (Resistor R2), and \(p4 = +30 W\) (Load Element). Element 5 is a Current-Controlled Voltage Source (CCVS) with terminal voltage \(v5 = 3.5 i_{x}\) (where \(i_{x} = 4 A\)) and branch current i5 entering its positive terminal. Find the branch current i5 and verify energy conservation.

Given parameters

  • \(p1 = -205 W, p2 = +60 W, p3 = +45 W, p4 = +30 W\)
  • Control current \(i_{x} = 4 A =>\) Dependent voltage \(v5 = 3.5 \times 4 = 14 V\)
Step-by-step solution
Step 1: Apply Conservation of Power \((\sum p_{k} = 0)\)

The algebraic sum of instantaneous power for all five elements must equal zero:

\(p1 + p2 + p3 + p4 + p5 = 0 => -205 + 60 + 45 + 30 + p5\)

\(= 0\)

Step 2: Solve for p5

Isolate the power of the fifth element:

\(-70 + p5 = 0 => p5\)

\(= +70 W\) (Absorbing Power)

Step 3: Determine branch current i5

Since i5 enters the positive terminal of Element \(5 (v5 = 14 V)\):

\(p5 = +v5 \cdot i5 => 70\)

\(= 14 \cdot i5 => i5\)

\(= +5.0 A\)

∴ \(p5 = +70 W\) (Absorbed), \(i5 = 5.0 A, \sum p = 0 W\) verified.

RMS and Average Values for Non-Sinusoidal & Periodic Waveforms

★★★ 06. Core Theory & Concept · Professor-Followed Book · B. L. Theraja & Alexander & Sadiku Ch. 1

  • For any periodic waveform \(x(t)\) with period \(T\): Average value \(X_{avg} = (\frac{1}{T}) \int x(t) dt\) and Effective (RMS) value \(X_{rms} = \sqrt{(\frac{1}{T}) \int x^{2}(t) dt}\).
  • An alternating or non-sinusoidal periodic waveform \(x(t) = x(t + T)\) is characterized by its average (DC) component and its Root-Mean-Square (RMS or effective) value, which represents the equivalent DC value that delivers the same average power to a resistor.
  • For a composite non-sinusoidal wave containing a DC term plus harmonics \(x(t) = X_{0} + X_{m1}\sin(\omega t) + X_{m2}\sin(2\omega t + \varphi _{2}) + \cdot \cdot \cdot \), the total RMS value is the square root of the sum of squares of the individual RMS components.
Full-Cycle Average (Mean) Value (\(V\) or \(A\))
\(X_{avg} = (\frac{1}{T}) \int _{0}^{T} x(t) dt\)
Effective / Root-Mean-Square (RMS) Value (\(V\) or \(A\))
\(X_{rms} = \sqrt{ (\frac{1}{T}) \int _{0}^{T} x^{2}(t) dt }\)
Multi-Harmonic Non-Sinusoidal RMS Value (Form Factor \(K_{f} = \frac{X_{rms}}{X_{avg}}\), Peak Factor \(K_{p} = \frac{X_{\max}}{X_{rms}}\))
\(X_{rms} = \sqrt{ X_{0}^{2} + (\frac{X_{m1}}{\sqrt{2}})^{2} + (\frac{X_{m2}}{\sqrt{2}})^{2} + \cdot \cdot \cdot }\)

Worked RMS & Average Value Calculation for Non-Sinusoidal Sawtooth & Composite Waveforms

★ 07. Worked Numerical Example · Syllabus Book · Robert L. Boylestad & R. C. Dorf & J. A. Svoboda

  • For a periodic sawtooth wave \(v(t) = (\frac{V_{m}}{T})t\) on \([0, T], V_{avg} = \frac{V_{m}}{2}\) and \(V_{rms} = \frac{V_{m}}{\sqrt{3}}\).
Problem / Theorem Question
  • (1) Derive the average value \(V_{avg}\) and effective (RMS) value \(V_{rms}\) of a periodic sawtooth voltage \(v(t) = (\frac{V_{m}}{T})t\) for \(0 \le t < T\).
  • (2) Calculate the total RMS current of \(i(t) = 3 + 4\sqrt{2} \sin(\omega t) + 3\sqrt{2} \cos(3\omega t) A\).
Step-by-step solution
1. Sawtooth Waveform Average & RMS Derivation

Integrate \(v(t)\) and \(v^{2}(t)\) over one period [0, T]:

\(V_{avg} = (\frac{1}{T}) \int _{0}^{T} (\frac{V_{m}}{T})t dt\)

\(= (\frac{V_{m}}{T^{2}}) [ \frac{t^{2}}{2} ]_{0}^{T}\)

∴ \(V_{avg} = \frac{V_{m}}{2} = 0.5 V_{m}\)

\(V_{rms} = \sqrt{ (\frac{1}{T}) \int _{0}^{T} (\frac{V_{m}^{2}}{T^{2}})t^{2} dt }\)

\(= \sqrt{ (\frac{V_{m}^{2}}{T^{3}}) (\frac{T^{3}}{3}) }\)

∴ \(V_{rms} = \frac{V_{m}}{\sqrt{3}} \approx 0.577 V_{m}\)

2. Multi-Harmonic RMS Current Calculation

Combine the DC term and the RMS values of each orthogonal harmonic:

\(I_{0} = 3 A, I_{rms,1} = \frac{4\sqrt{2}}{\sqrt{2}}\)

\(= 4 A, I_{rms,3} = \frac{3\sqrt{2}}{\sqrt{2}}\)

\(= 3 A\)

\(I_{rms} = \sqrt{I_{0}^{2} + I_{rms,1}^{2} + I_{rms,3}^{2}}\)

\(= \sqrt{3^{2} + 4^{2} + 3^{2}}\)

∴ \(I_{rms} = \sqrt{34} \approx 5.83 A\)

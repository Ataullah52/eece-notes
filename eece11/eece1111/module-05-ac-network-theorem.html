Maximum Average Power Transfer Theorem in AC Sinusoidal Networks

P1 · Class Note · Core Theory & Concept · Class Note · AC Network Theorems & Complex Conjugate Matching

For a complex load impedance \(Z_{L} = R_{L} + jX_{L}\) connected to an AC Thevenin network \(Z_{T}h = R_{T}h + jX_{T}h\), maximum average power occurs when \(Z_{L} = Z_{T}h* = R_{T}h - jX_{T}h\).

In sinusoidal steady-state AC circuits, all DC network theorems (Superposition, Thevenin, Norton, Millman, Substitution, and Reciprocity) extend directly to the frequency domain by replacing real voltages/currents with phasors (V, I) and resistances with complex impedances \((Z = R + jX)\).

For Maximum Average Power Transfer to a variable complex load \(Z_{L} = R_{L} + jX_{L}\), the load reactance must first cancel the Thevenin reactance \((X_{L} = -X_{T}h)\) and the load resistance must match the Thevenin resistance \((R_{L} = R_{T}h)\).

Complex Conjugate Impedance Matching Condition (Ohms \((\Omega )\))
\(Z_{L} = Z_{Th}^* ⟺ R_{L} = R_{Th}\) and \(X_{L} = -X_{Th}\)
Maximum Average AC Power Transferred (using RMS \(V_{T}h\)) (Watts (W))
\(P_{\max} = |V_\{Th\),rms\(\}|\frac{^{2}}{4 \cdot R_{Th}} = |V_\{Th\),peak\(\}|\frac{^{2}}{8 \cdot R_{Th}}\)
Purely Resistive Load Restriction \((X_{L} = 0)\) (Ohms \((\Omega )\) when load reactance cannot be varied)
\(R_{L} = |Z_{Th}| = \sqrt{R_{Th}^{2} + X_{Th}^{2}}\)

AC Thevenin Equivalent & Maximum Average Power Delivered to Load \(Z_{L}\)

P2 · Previous Year Question · Previous Year Question · Previous Year Question · PUST EECE Final Exam 2022 Q5(b) & 2021 Q5(c) · Asked in: 2022, 2021 (6 Marks)

Given \(Z_{T}h = 4 + j3 \Omega \) and \(V_{T}h = 20\angle 0^{\circ} V\) (RMS), find \(Z_{L}\) for maximum average power and compute \(P_{\max}\).

Problem / Theorem Question

An AC two-terminal network has a Thevenin open-circuit RMS voltage \(V_{T}h = 20\angle 0^{\circ} V\) and Thevenin impedance \(Z_{T}h = 4 + j3 \Omega \). Determine (a) the complex load impedance \(Z_{L}\) for maximum average power transfer and \(P_{\max}\)

(b) the value of a purely resistive load \(R_{L}\) for maximum power.

Step-by-step solution
Part (a) & Part (b): Complex Conjugate vs. Purely Resistive Load

Apply \(Z_{L} = Z_{T}h*\) for Part (a) and \(R_{L} = |Z_{T}h|\) for Part (b):

(a) \(Z_{L} = Z_{Th}^*\)

\(= 4 - j3 \Omega \)

\(P_{\max} = |V_\{Th\),rms\(\}|\frac{^{2}}{4 R_{Th}} = \frac{20^{2}}{4 \times 4}\)

∴ \(Z_{L} = 4 - j3 \Omega , P_{\max} = 25 W\)

(b) For purely resistive load \((X_{L} = 0)\):

\(R_{L} = \sqrt{4^{2} + 3^{2}}\)

∴ \(R_{L} = 5 \Omega \)

AC Superposition with Multi-Frequency Sources

P3 · Professor-Followed Book · Core Theory & Concept · Professor-Followed Book · Alexander & Sadiku (Ch. 10 & Ch. 11)

When AC sources operate at different angular frequencies \(\omega _{1} \neq \omega _{2}\), phasors cannot be added directly; sum the time-domain responses.

Multi-Frequency Superposition Rule
\(v(t) = v_{DC} + v_{1}(t; \omega _{1}) + v_{2}(t; \omega _{2})\)

AC Millman’s & Norton Phasor Equivalence in Parallel Impedance Branches

P4 · Syllabus Book · Worked Numerical Example · Syllabus Book · Corcoran & Kerchner (Alternating-Current Circuits)

Convert parallel AC voltage branches into Norton phasor currents \(I_{N} = V_{k} Y_{k}\) and sum admittances \(Y_{eq} = \sum Y_{k}\).

AC Phasor Millman Formula
\(V_{eq} = \frac{\sum V_{k} Y_{k}}{\sum Y_{k}}\), where \(Y_{k} = \frac{1}{Z_{k}}\)

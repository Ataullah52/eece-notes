PUST Past Exam Questions on Reduction of Central Conics (Hyperbola) & Parabola to Standard Form

P2 · Previous Year Question + P1 · Previous Year Question · Previous Year Question · PUST EECE 2022–23, 2021–22 & 2020–21 Semester Final Exams [7 Marks] · Asked in: 2022–23, 2021–22, 2020–21 (7 Marks)

Complete exam-style vertical derivations for reducing general second-degree equations to canonical hyperbola and parabola forms.

Problem / Theorem Question

(1) [PUST Past Exam — 7 Marks] Reduce \(11x^{2} + 24xy + 4y^{2} - 20x - 40y - 5 = 0\) to standard form. Identify the conic and find its center, lengths of axes, eccentricity, latus rectum, and equations of the axes.

(2) [PUST Past Exam — 7 Marks] Reduce \(9x^{2} - 24xy + 16y^{2} - 18x - 101y + 19 = 0\) to standard form, and find its vertex, focus, axis, latus rectum, and directrix.

Step-by-step solution
1. Central Conic Reduction: \(11x^{2} + 24xy + 4y^{2} - 20x - 40y - 5 = 0\)

Here \(a = 11, h = 12, b = 4, g = -10, f = -20, c = -5\), so ab \(- h^{2} = 44 - 144 = -100 < 0\) (Hyperbola):

Step 1 (Center \((\alpha , \beta )\) and New Constant c′):

\(11\alpha + 12\beta - 10 = 0\) and \(3\alpha + \beta = 5\)

\(\implies \) Center \((\alpha , \beta ) = (2, -1), c' = g\alpha + f\beta + c\)

\(= -5\)

Step 2 (Characteristic Roots & Standard Form):

\(\lambda ^{2} - 15\lambda - 100 = 0\)

\(\implies \lambda _{1} = 20, \lambda _{2} = -5\)

\(\implies 20X^{2} - 5Y^{2} - 5 = 0\)

∴ \(\frac{X^{2}}{\frac{1}{4}} - \frac{Y^{2}}{1} = 1\) [Hyperbola with \(A = \frac{1}{2}, B = 1\)]

Step 3 (Axes, Eccentricity & Latus Rectum):

• Eccentricity \(e = \sqrt{1 + \frac{B^{2}}{A^{2}}} = \sqrt{5}\); Latus rectum \(= \frac{2B^{2}}{A} = 4\)

∴ Center: \((2, -1), e = \sqrt{5}, LR = 4\), Axes: \(3x - 4y - 10 = 0\) and \(4x + 3y - 5 = 0\)

2. Parabola Reduction (ab \(- h^{2} = 0): 9x^{2} - 24xy + 16y^{2} - 18x - 101y + 19 = 0\)

Write \((3x - 4y + \lambda )^{2} = (6\lambda + 18)x + (101 - 8\lambda )y + (\lambda ^{2} - 19)\) and enforce orthogonality:

For perpendicular lines:

\(3(6\lambda + 18) - 4(101 - 8\lambda ) = 0\)

\(\implies 50\lambda = 350\)

\(\implies \lambda = 7\)

Normalizing by \(5^{2} = 25\):

\([\frac{3x - 4y + 7}{5}]^{2} = 3 \cdot [\frac{4x + 3y + 2}{5}]\)

∴ \(Y^{2} = 3X\) (Parabola with \(4a = 3, a = \frac{3}{4}\))

∴ Vertex: \((-\frac{29}{25}, \frac{22}{25})\), Focus: \((-\frac{14}{25}, \frac{43}{100})\), Directrix: \(16x + 12y + 23 = 0\)

PUST Past Exam Questions on Orthogonal Circles, Radical Axis & Limiting Points of Coaxial Systems

P2 · Previous Year Question + P1 · Previous Year Question · Previous Year Question · PUST EECE 2022–23, 2021–22 & 2020–21 Semester Final Exams [5 Marks] · Asked in: 2022–23, 2021–22, 2020–21 (5 Marks)

Solve \(2gg_{i} + 2ff_{i} = c + c_{i}\) for the orthogonal circle, and set radius \(R = 0\) in \(S_{1} + \lambda (S_{1} - S_{2}) = 0\) for limiting points.

Problem / Theorem Question

(1) [PUST Past Exam — 5 Marks] Find the equation of the circle that cuts orthogonally the three circles \(x^{2} + y^{2} + 2x + 17y + 4 = 0, x^{2} + y^{2} + 7x + 6y + 11 = 0\), and \(x^{2} + y^{2} - x + 22y + 3 = 0\).

(2) [PUST Past Exam — 5 Marks] Find the radical axis and the limiting points of the coaxial system determined by \(S_{1} \equiv x^{2} + y^{2} + 2x + 4y + 7 = 0\) and \(S_{2} \equiv x^{2} + y^{2} + 4x + 2y + 5 = 0\).

Step-by-step solution
1. Circle Orthogonal to Three Given Circles

Let the required circle be \(x^{2} + y^{2} + 2gx + 2fy + c = 0\) and apply \(2gg_{i} + 2ff_{i} = c + c_{i}\):

\(2g + 17f - c = 4 --- (1)\)

\(7g + 6f - c = 11 --- (2)\)

\(-g + 22f - c = 3 --- (3)\)

Subtracting (1) from (2), and (3) from (1):

\(5g - 11f = 7\) and \(3g - 5f = 1\)

\(\implies g = -3, f = -2, c = -44\)

∴ \(x^{2} + y^{2} - 6x - 4y - 44 = 0\)

2. Radical Axis & Limiting Points of a Coaxial System

Form \(S_{1} + \frac{2\lambda (S_{1} - S_{2})}{2} = 0\) and set radius \(R^{2} = 0\):

Radical axis: \(S_{1} - S_{2} = 0\)

\(\implies L \equiv x - y - 1 = 0\)

Coaxial family \(S_{1} + 2\lambda L = 0\):

\(x^{2} + y^{2} + 2(1 + \lambda )x + 2(2 - \lambda )y + (7 - 2\lambda ) = 0\)

Center \(C = (-(1 + \lambda ), -(2 - \lambda ))\), Radius \(R^{2} = 2\lambda ^{2} - 2\)

\(= 0\)

\(\implies \lambda = \pm 1\)

∴ Radical Axis: \(x - y - 1 = 0\); Limiting Points: (−2, −1) and (0, −3)

Rahman & Bhattacharjee & Anton: Reduction of Ellipse to Standard Form & Polar Equations of Conics

P3 · Professor-Followed Book · Worked Numerical Example · Professor-Followed Book · Rahman & Bhattacharjee (Ch. 4) & Howard Anton (Sec. 10.6)

When ab \(- h^{2} > 0\) and \(\lambda _{1}, \lambda _{2}\) have the same sign opposite to c′, the second-degree equation reduces to a real ellipse \(\frac{X^{2}}{A^{2}} + \frac{Y^{2}}{B^{2}} = 1\).

Polar Equation of a Conic (Focus at the Pole)
\(\frac{l}{r} = 1 + e \cos \theta \) (where \(l =\) ed is the semi-latus rectum)
Problem / Theorem Question

(1) [Rahman & Bhattacharjee] Reduce \(5x^{2} - 6xy + 5y^{2} + 18x - 14y + 9 = 0\) to standard form and find its center, lengths of axes, eccentricity, and equations of axes.

(2) [Anton 10.6] Identify and find the directrix and vertices of the polar conic \(r = \frac{6}{2 + \cos \theta }\).

Step-by-step solution
1. Ellipse Reduction: \(5x^{2} - 6xy + 5y^{2} + 18x - 14y + 9 = 0\)

Here \(a = 5, h = -3, b = 5, g = 9, f = -7, c = 9\), so ab \(- h^{2} = 25 - 9 = 16 > 0\) (Ellipse):

Center equations:

\(5\alpha - 3\beta + 9 = 0\) and \(-3\alpha + 5\beta - 7 = 0\)

\(\implies \) Center \((\alpha , \beta ) = (-\frac{3}{2}, \frac{1}{2})\)

\(c' = g\alpha + f\beta + c\)

\(= 9(-\frac{3}{2}) + (-7)(\frac{1}{2}) + 9\)

\(= -8\)

Characteristic equation:

\(\lambda ^{2} - 10\lambda + 16 = 0\)

\(\implies \lambda _{1} = 2, \lambda _{2} = 8\)

\(\implies 2X^{2} + 8Y^{2} - 8 = 0\)

∴ \(\frac{X^{2}}{4} + \frac{Y^{2}}{1} = 1\) (Ellipse with \(A = 2, B = 1, e = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\))

2. Polar Conic Analysis: \(r = \frac{6}{2 + \cos \theta }\)

Divide numerator and denominator by 2 to match \(\frac{l}{r} = 1 + e \cos \theta \):

\(r = \frac{3}{1 + (\frac{1}{2}) \cos \theta }\)

\(\implies \) Eccentricity \(e = \frac{1}{2} < 1\) (Ellipse), \(l =\) ed

\(= 3 \implies \) Directrix distance \(d = 6\)

Vertices at \(\theta = 0\) and \(\theta = \pi \):

\(r(0) = \frac{3}{\frac{3}{2}}\)

\(= 2, r(\pi ) = \frac{3}{\frac{1}{2}}\)

\(= 6\)

∴ Ellipse with \(e = \frac{1}{2}\), major axis \(2a = 2 + 6 = 8\), directrix \(x = 6\)

Syllabus Book Problems (Askwith): Radical Center of Three Circles & Coaxial System from Limiting Points

P4 · Syllabus Book · Worked Numerical Example · Syllabus Book · E. H. Askwith (Analytic Geometry of Conic Sections, Ch. V)

If \((\alpha _{1}, \beta _{1})\) and \((\alpha _{2}, \beta _{2})\) are the limiting points (point-circles \(R = 0\)) of a coaxial system, the system is \(S_{1} + \lambda S_{2} = 0\) where \(S_{i} = (x - \alpha _{i})^{2} + (y - \beta _{i})^{2} = 0\).

Problem / Theorem Question

(1) [E. H. Askwith] Find the Radical Center of the three circles \(x^{2} + y^{2} - 2x - 4y + 1 = 0, x^{2} + y^{2} - 4x - 2y + 1 = 0\), and \(x^{2} + y^{2} + 2x - 6y - 3 = 0\).

(2) Find the equation of the coaxial system of circles whose limiting points are (1, −1) and (3, 1).

Step-by-step solution
1. Radical Center of Three Given Circles

Find the intersection of the radical axes \(S_{1} - S_{2} = 0\) and \(S_{2} - S_{3} = 0\):

Radical axis \(S_{1} - S_{2} = 0\):

\(2x - 2y = 0\)

\(\implies x = y\)

Radical axis \(S_{2} - S_{3} = 0\):

\(-6x + 4y + 4 = 0\)

\(\implies 3x - 2y = 2\)

Substituting \(y = x\) gives:

\(3x - 2x = 2\)

\(\implies x = 2, y = 2\)

∴ Radical Center \(= (2, 2)\)

2. Coaxial System Given Two Limiting Points (1, −1) and (3, 1)

Treat each limiting point as a point-circle of zero radius:

Point-circle \(S_{1}\) at \((1, -1): (x - 1)^{2} + (y + 1)^{2} = x^{2} + y^{2} - 2x + 2y + 2\)

\(= 0\)

Point-circle \(S_{2}\) at \((3, 1): (x - 3)^{2} + (y - 1)^{2} = x^{2} + y^{2} - 6x - 2y + 10\)

\(= 0\)

Radical axis \(S_{1} - S_{2} = 0\):

\(4x + 4y - 8 = 0 \iff x + y - 2 = 0\)

∴ Coaxial Family: \(x^{2} + y^{2} - 2x + 2y + 2 + \lambda (x + y - 2) = 0\)

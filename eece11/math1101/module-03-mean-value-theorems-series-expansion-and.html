Rolle’s Theorem, Lagrange’s MVT, Cauchy’s MVT & Class Handout Solutions

★★★★★ 01. Core Theory & Concept · Class Note · Units 6–7 & Professor’s Rolle’s / MVT Handout (#2, #5, #6, #9)

  • Rolle’s Theorem: \(f(a) = f(b) \implies f'(c) = 0\) for some \(c \in (a, b)\).
  • Lagrange’s MVT: \(f'(c) = \frac{f(b) - f(a)}{b - a}\) for some \(c \in (a, b)\).
Rolle’s Theorem Condition & Conclusion
Continuous on [a, b], Differentiable on \((a, b), f(a) = f(b) \implies \exists c \in (a, b) : f'(c) = 0\)
Lagrange’s Mean Value Theorem (MVT)
Continuous on [a, b], Differentiable on \((a, b) \implies \exists c \in (a, b) : f'(c) = \frac{f(b) - f(a)}{b - a}\)
Cauchy’s Generalized Mean Value Theorem
\(f'\frac{c}{g'(c)} = \frac{f(b) - f(a)}{g(b) - g(a)}\) (where \(g'(x) \neq 0\) on (a, b))

1. Proofs of Rolle’s Theorem (22–23 Q2a) & Lagrange’s MVT (21–22 Q1b)

Use Fermat’s interior extremum theorem for Rolle’s Theorem, and auxiliary function \(\varphi (x) = f(x) +\) Ax for MVT:

Rolle’s Theorem Proof:

• Case \(1 (M = m): f(x)\) is constant on [a, b], so \(f'(c) = 0\) for all \(c \in (a, b)\).

• Case \(2 (M > m)\): Since \(f(a) = f(b)\), at least one extremum occurs at an interior point \(c \in (a, b)\),

where Fermat’s Theorem gives:

∴ \(f'(c) = 0\) for some \(c \in (a, b)\)

Lagrange’s MVT Proof:

Let \(\varphi (x) = f(x) +\) Ax with \(\varphi (a)\)

\(= \varphi (b)\)

\(\implies A = -\frac{f(b) - f(a)}{b - a}\).

Applying Rolle’s Theorem to \(\varphi (x)\) gives \(\varphi '(c) = f'(c) + A\)

\(= 0\):

∴ \(f'(c) = \frac{f(b) - f(a)}{b - a}\) for some \(c \in (a, b)\)

1. Anton 3.6 Ex. 66: Unknown Parameters \(k\) and \(l\)

Enforce numerator \(\to 0\) at \(x = 0\) for finite limit existence:

Given, \(\lim\limits_{x\to 0} (k + \cos\) lx) \(/ x^{2} = -4\)

Since denominator \(x^{2} \to 0\), numerator must also approach 0:

\(k + \cos 0 = 0\)

\(\implies k = -1\)

Now apply L’Hôpital’s Rule:

\(\lim\limits_{x\to 0} (-1 + \cos\) lx) \(/ x^{2} = \lim\limits_{x\to 0} (-l \sin\) lx) / (2x)

\(= -\frac{l^{2}}{2}\)

\(= -4\)

\(\implies l^{2} = 8\)

∴ \(k = -1, l = \pm 2\sqrt{2}\)

2. Anton 9.8 Ex. 45: Radius & Interval of Convergence

Apply the Ratio Test and check both endpoints \(x = -2\) and \(x = 0\):

\(u_{k} = (-1)^{k+1} \frac{(x + 1)^{k}}{k}\)

\(\lim\limits_{k\to \infty } |\frac{u_{k+1}}{u_{k}}| = \lim\limits_{k\to \infty } [\frac{k}{k + 1}] \cdot |x + 1|\)

\(= |x + 1| < 1\)

\(\implies \) Radius of convergence \(R = 1\), and \(-2 < x < 0\)

Endpoint checks:

• At \(x = -2: \sum (-1)\frac{^{2k+1}}{k} = -\sum \frac{1}{k}\) (Diverges, harmonic series)

• At \(x = 0: \sum (-1)\frac{^{k+1}}{k}\) (Converges, alternating harmonic series)

∴ Radius \(R = 1\), Interval of Convergence \(I = (-2, 0]\)

Problem / Theorem Question

  • (1) Cissoid of Diocles: \(y^{2}(2a - x) = x^{3} (a > 0)\)
  • (2) Looping Cubic Curve: \(a y^{2} = x^{2}(a - x) (a > 0)\).
1. Cissoid of Diocles: \(y^{2}(2a - x) = x^{3} (a > 0)\)

Apply the 4-step curve tracing procedure:

1. Symmetry: Only even power of \(y (y^{2})\) appears

\(\implies \) Symmetric about the x-axis.

2. Origin & Tangent: Passes through (0, 0). Lowest degree terms: \(2ay^{2} = 0\)

\(\implies y = 0, 0\)

\(\implies \) Origin (0, 0) is a cusp with the x-axis as double tangent.

3. Asymptote: Coefficient of \(y^{2}\) is \(2a - x = 0\)

\(\implies x = 2a\) is the vertical asymptote.

4. Excluded Region: \(y^{2} = \frac{x^{3}}{2a - x} < 0\) when \(x < 0\) or \(x > 2a\).

∴ Symmetric about x-axis, cusp at (0, 0), asymptote \(x = 2a\), exists on \(0 \le x < 2a\)

2. Nodal Cubic Curve: \(a y^{2} = x^{2}(a - x) (a > 0)\)

Identify the node at (0, 0) and loop between \(x = 0\) and \(x = a\):

1. Symmetry: Even power of \(y (y^{2})\)

\(\implies \) Symmetric about the x-axis.

2. Origin & Tangents: Lowest degree terms \(a(y^{2} - x^{2}) = 0\)

\(\implies y = \pm x\)

\(\implies \) Origin (0, 0) is a Node with two distinct tangents \(y = +x\) and \(y = -x\).

3. Intercepts: Meets x-axis at (0, 0) and (a, 0) with vertical tangent at (a, 0).

4. Region: \(y^{2} = \frac{x^{2}(a - x)}{a} < 0\) for \(x > a\) (no curve for \(x > a\)).

∴ Closed loop between \(x = 0\) and \(x = a\), node at (0, 0) with tangents \(y = \pm x\)

Problem / Theorem Question
  • Find the n-th derivative \(y_{n}\) for:
  • (1) \(y = \frac{x}{(x - 1)(x - 2)(x - 3)}\)
  • (2) \(y = \sin^{3}x\) and \(y = \sin x \cdot \sin 3x\)
  • (3) if \(y =\) a \(\cos(\ln x) + b \sin(\ln x)\), show that \(x^{2} y_{n+2} + (2n + 1)x y_{n+1} + (n^{2} + 1)y_{n} = 0\).
1. Partial Fraction Decomposition: \(y = \frac{x}{(x - 1)(x - 2)(x - 3)}\)

Use the cover-up rule to find A, B, C and apply \(D^{n}[\frac{1}{x - k}]\):

Let \(\frac{x}{(x - 1)(x - 2)(x - 3)} = \frac{A}{x - 1} + \frac{B}{x - 2} + \frac{C}{x - 3}\)

For \(x = 1: A = \frac{1}{(1 - 2)(1 - 3)}\)

\(= +\frac{1}{2}\)

For \(x = 2: B = \frac{2}{(2 - 1)(2 - 3)}\)

\(= -2\)

For \(x = 3: C = \frac{3}{(3 - 1)(3 - 2)}\)

\(= +\frac{3}{2}\)

\(\implies y = \frac{1}{2(x - 1)} - \frac{2}{x - 2} + \frac{3}{2(x - 3)}\)

∴ \(y_{n} = (-1)^{n} n! [ \frac{1}{2(x - 1)^{n+1}} - \frac{2}{(x - 2)^{n+1}} + \frac{3}{2(x - 3)^{n+1}} ]\)

2. Trigonometric Reduction: (a) \(y = \sin^{3}x\) and (b) \(y = \sin x \sin 3x\)

Linearize powers and products of sines/cosines before differentiating:

(a) Given, \(y = \sin^{3}x\)

\(\implies y = (\frac{1}{4})(3 \sin x - \sin 3x) [∵ \sin 3x = 3 \sin x - 4 \sin^{3}x]\)

∴ \(y_{n} = (\frac{3}{4}) \sin(x + \frac{n\pi }{2}) - (\frac{3^{n}}{4}) \sin(3x + \frac{n\pi }{2})\)

(b) Given, \(y = \sin x \sin 3x\)

\(\implies y = (\frac{1}{2})(\cos 2x - \cos 4x) [∵ 2 \sin\) A \(\sin B = \cos(A - B) - \cos(A + B)\)]

∴ \(y_{n} = (\frac{1}{2})[ 2^{n} \cos(2x + \frac{n\pi }{2}) - 4^{n} \cos(4x + \frac{n\pi }{2}) ]\)

3. Class Lecture Problem: \(y =\) a \(\cos(\ln x) + b \sin(\ln x)\)

Multiply by \(x\) before differentiating a second time:

Given, \(y =\) a \(\cos(\ln x) + b \sin(\ln x)\)

\(\implies y_{1} = [-\)a \(\sin(\ln x) + b \cos(\ln x)\frac{]}{x}\)

\(\implies x y_{1} = -\)a \(\sin(\ln x) + b \cos(\ln x)\)

\(\implies x y_{2} + y_{1} = [-\)a \(\cos(\ln x) - b \sin(\ln x)\frac{]}{x}\)

\(\implies x y_{2} + y_{1} = -\frac{y}{x}\)

\(\implies x^{2} y_{2} + x y_{1} + y = 0\)

Applying Leibniz’s Theorem \((D^{n})\):

∴ \(x^{2} y_{n+2} + (2n + 1)x y_{n+1} + (n^{2} + 1)y_{n} = 0\)

PUST Past Exam Questions on Differentiability at a Point & Continuity vs. Differentiability

★★★★ 02. Previous Year Question (PYQ) · Previous Year Question · PUST EECE 2022–23 Q1(c) [5.5 Marks], 2021–22 Q2(a) [5 Marks] · Asked in: 2022–23, 2021–22 (5–5.5 Marks)

  • \(f(x)\) is differentiable at \(x =\) a iff L.H.D. \(=\) R.H.D. (both finite).
  • Every differentiable function is continuous, but the converse is false.
Left-Hand & Right-Hand Derivatives at \(x = a\)
Lf\('(a) = \lim\limits_{h\to 0^{-}} \frac{f(a + h) - f(a)}{h}\)Rf\('(a) = \lim\limits_{h\to 0^{+}} \frac{f(a + h) - f(a)}{h}\)
Slope of Tangent Line & Non-Differentiable Points
\(m_{t}an = \tan \theta = f'(a)\)Fails at: (1) Corner (LHD \(\neq \) RHD), (2) Vertical Tangent \((f' = \pm \infty ), (3)\) Cusp
Problem / Theorem Question
  • (1) Prove that differentiability implies continuity.
  • (2) [PUST EECE 2022–23 Q1(c) — 5.5 Marks] Prove that \(f(x) = |x|\) is continuous at \(x = 0\), but not differentiable at \(x = 0\).
  • (3) [PUST EECE 2021–22 Q2(a) — 5 Marks] Discuss the continuity and differentiability of \(f(x) = \{ 2x (x < 0); 2x^{2} (0 \le x < 1); 4 - 2x (x \ge 1) \}\) at \(x = 1\).
1. Theorem Proof: Differentiability Implies Continuity

Assume \(f'(a) = \lim\limits_{x\to a} \frac{f(x) - f(a)}{x - a}\) exists finitely:

\(\lim\limits_{x\to a} [f(x) - f(a)]\)

\(= \lim\limits_{x\to a} [ (\frac{f(x) - f(a)}{x - a}) \cdot (x - a) ]\)

\(= [ \lim\limits_{x\to a} \frac{f(x) - f(a)}{x - a} ] \cdot [ \lim\limits_{x\to a} (x - a) ]\)

\(= f'(a) \cdot 0\)

\(= 0\)

\(\implies \lim\limits_{x\to a} f(x) = f(a)\)

∴ Every function differentiable at \(x =\) a is continuous at \(x = a\).

2. PUST EECE 2022–23 Q1(c) [5.5 Marks]: \(f(x) = |x|\) at \(x = 0\)

Test continuity and one-sided derivatives at \(x = 0\):

Step 1 (Continuity at \(x = 0\)):

\(f(0) = 0\)

L.H.L. \(= \lim\limits_{x\to 0^{-}} (-x)\)

\(= 0\)

R.H.L. \(= \lim\limits_{x\to 0^{+}} (+x)\)

\(= 0\)

Since L.H.L. \(=\) R.H.L.

\(= f(0)\)

\(= 0, f(x) = |x|\) is continuous at \(x = 0\).

Step 2 (Differentiability at \(x = 0\)):

L.H.D. \(= \lim\limits_{h\to 0^{-}} \frac{f(0 + h) - f(0)}{h}\)

\(= \lim\limits_{h\to 0^{-}} \frac{-h}{h}\)

\(= -1\)

R.H.D. \(= \lim\limits_{h\to 0^{+}} \frac{f(0 + h) - f(0)}{h}\)

\(= \lim\limits_{h\to 0^{+}} \frac{+h}{h}\)

\(= +1\)

∴ Since L.H.D. \((-1) \neq \) R.H.D. \((+1), f(x) = |x|\) is continuous but not differentiable at \(x = 0\).

3. PUST EECE 2021–22 Q2(a) [5 Marks]: Piecewise Continuity & Differentiability at \(x = 1\)

Evaluate L.H.L., R.H.L., L.H.D., and R.H.D. at \(x = 1\):

Step 1 (Continuity at \(x = 1\)):

\(f(1) = 4 - 2(1)\)

\(= 2\)

L.H.L. \(= \lim\limits_{x\to 1^{-}} (2x^{2})\)

\(= 2\)

R.H.L. \(= \lim\limits_{x\to 1^{+}} (4 - 2x)\)

\(= 2\)

Since L.H.L. \(=\) R.H.L.

\(= f(1)\)

\(= 2, f(x)\) is continuous at \(x = 1\).

Step 2 (Differentiability at \(x = 1\)):

L.H.D. \(= \lim\limits_{h\to 0^{-}} \frac{2(1 + h)^{2} - 2}{h}\)

\(= \lim\limits_{h\to 0^{-}} \frac{4h + 2h^{2}}{h}\)

\(= 4\)

R.H.D. \(= \lim\limits_{h\to 0^{+}} \frac{4 - 2(1 + h) - 2}{h}\)

\(= \lim\limits_{h\to 0^{+}} \frac{-2h}{h}\)

\(= -2\)

∴ \(f(x)\) is continuous at \(x = 1\), but not differentiable at \(x = 1 (∵\) L.H.D. \(= 4 \neq \) R.H.D. \(= -2\)).

PUST Past Exam Questions on Leibniz’s Theorem: Induction Proof, Differential Equations \(\& y_{n}(0)\)

★★★★★ 03. Previous Year Question (PYQ) · Previous Year Question · PUST EECE 2022–23 Q2(b) [4.5 Marks], Q4(c) [5 Marks], 2021–22 Q2(b) [5 Marks], 2020–21 Q2(a) [5 Marks] · Asked in: 2022–23, 2021–22, 2020–21 (4.5–5 Marks)

  • (uv)\(_{n} = u_{n} v + ^{n}C_{1} u_{n-1} v_{1} + ^{n}C_{2} u_{n-2} v_{2} + \cdot \cdot \cdot + ^{n}C_{r} u_{n-r} v_{r} + \cdot \cdot \cdot + u v_{n}\).
Leibniz’s Theorem Formula
\((u \cdot v)_{n} = u_{n} v + ^{n}C_{1} u_{n-1} v_{1} + ^{n}C_{2} u_{n-2} v_{2} + \cdot \cdot \cdot + ^{n}C_{r} u_{n-r} v_{r} + \cdot \cdot \cdot + u v_{n}\)
Standard Leibniz Term Expansions
\(D^{n}[(1 - x^{2})y_{2}] = (1 - x^{2})y_{n+2} - 2nx y_{n+1} - n(n - 1)y_{n}\)\(D^{n}[x y_{1}] = x y_{n+1} + n y_{n}\)
  • Always eliminate radicals by cross-multiplying and squaring \(y_{1}\) before differentiating for \(y_{2}\)
Problem / Theorem Question
  • (1) [PUST 2022–23 Q2b] State and prove Leibniz’s Theorem by mathematical induction.
  • (2) [PUST 2020–21 Q2a] If \(y = \sin(m \sin^{-1}x)\), show that \((1 - x^{2})y_{n+2} - (2n + 1)xy_{n+1} + (m^{2} - n^{2})y_{n} = 0\) and find \(y_{n}(0)\).
  • (3) [PUST 2022–23 Q4c] If \(y = e^{a \sin^{-1}x}\), show that \((1 - x^{2})y_{n+2} - (2n + 1)xy_{n+1} - (n^{2} + a^{2})y_{n} = 0\) and find \(y_{n}(0)\).
  • (4) [PUST 2021–22 Q2b] If \(y = (x^{2} - 1)^{n}\), prove \((x^{2} - 1)y_{n+2} + 2xy_{n+1} - n(n + 1)y_{n} = 0\).
1. Proof of Leibniz’s Theorem by Mathematical Induction (PUST 2022–23 Q2b)

Verify base step \(n = 1, 2\) and inductive step \(n = m \to m + 1\) using Pascal’s identity:

Step 1 (Base Step \(n = 1\)):

(uv)\(_{1} = u_{1}v + u v_{1}\)

\(= u_{1}v + ^{1}C_{1} u v_{1}\) (Holds for \(n = 1\))

Step 2 (Inductive Hypothesis for \(n = m\)):

Assume (uv)\(_{m} = u_{m} v + ^{m}C_{1} u_{m-1} v_{1} + \cdot \cdot \cdot + ^{m}C_{r} u_{m-r} v_{r} + \cdot \cdot \cdot + u v_{m}\)

Step 3 (Inductive Step for \(n = m + 1\)):

Differentiating w.r.t. \(x\) and \(using ^{m}C_{r-1} + ^{m}C_{r} = ^{m+1}C_{r}\):

(uv)\(_{m+1} = u_{m+1}v + [^{m}C_{0} + ^{m}C_{1}] u_{m} v_{1} + \cdot \cdot \cdot + [^{m}C_{r-1} + ^{m}C_{r}] u_{m+1-r} v_{r} + \cdot \cdot \cdot + u v_{m+1}\)

∴ (uv)\(_{m+1} = u_{m+1}v + ^{m+1}C_{1} u_{m} v_{1} + \cdot \cdot \cdot + ^{m+1}C_{r} u_{m+1-r} v_{r} + \cdot \cdot \cdot + u v_{m+1}\)

2. PUST 2020–21 Q2\((a): y = \sin(m \sin^{-1}x)\) and evaluation of \(y_{n}(0)\)

Cross-multiply \(\sqrt{1 - x^{2}}\), square both sides, differentiate, and apply Leibniz’s Theorem:

Given, \(y = \sin(m \sin^{-1}x)\)

\(\implies y_{1} = \frac{m \cos(m \sin^{-1}x)}{\sqrt{1 - x^{2}}}\)

\(\implies (1 - x^{2})y_{1}^{2} = m^{2}(1 - y^{2})\)

\(\implies (1 - x^{2})(2y_{1}y_{2}) - 2x y_{1}^{2} = -2m^{2} y y_{1}\)

\(\implies (1 - x^{2})y_{2} - x y_{1} + m^{2} y = 0\) [Dividing by \(2y_{1} \neq 0\)]

Applying Leibniz’s Theorem \((D^{n})\):

∴ \((1 - x^{2})y_{n+2} - (2n + 1)x y_{n+1} + (m^{2} - n^{2})y_{n} = 0\)

At \(x = 0: y(0) = 0, y_{1}(0) = m, y_{2}(0) = 0, y_{n+2}(0) = (n^{2} - m^{2}) y_{n}(0)\)

∴ \(y_{n}(0) = 0\) (even \(n); y_{n}(0) = m(1^{2} - m^{2})(3^{2} - m^{2})\cdot \cdot \cdot ((n - 2)^{2} - m^{2}\)) (odd \(n \ge 3\))

3. PUST 2022–23 Q4(c) & 2021–22 Q2\((b): y = e^{a \sin^{-1}x}\) and \(y = (x^{2} - 1)^{n}\)

Vertical derivations for exponential inverse-sine and Legendre polynomial forms:

Problem \(A\) (PUST 2022–23 Q4c): \(y = e^{a \sin^{-1}x}\)

\(\implies (1 - x^{2})y_{1}^{2} = a^{2} y^{2}\)

\(\implies (1 - x^{2})y_{2} - x y_{1} - a^{2} y = 0\)

∴ \((1 - x^{2})y_{n+2} - (2n + 1)x y_{n+1} - (n^{2} + a^{2})y_{n} = 0\)

∴ \(y_{n}(0) = a^{2}(2^{2} + a^{2})\cdot \cdot \cdot ((n - 2)^{2} + a^{2})\) [even \(n]; a(1^{2} + a^{2})\cdot \cdot \cdot ((n - 2)^{2} + a^{2}\)) [odd \(n\)]

Problem \(B\) (PUST 2021–22 Q2b): \(y = (x^{2} - 1)^{n}\)

\(\implies y_{1} = 2nx(x^{2} - 1)^{n-1}\)

\(\implies (x^{2} - 1)y_{1} = 2nxy\)

\(\implies (x^{2} - 1)y_{2} - 2(n - 1)xy_{1} - 2ny = 0\)

∴ \((x^{2} - 1)y_{n+2} + 2x y_{n+1} - n(n + 1)y_{n} = 0\)

Howard Anton Sec. 4.4: Absolute (Global) Maxima and Minima on a Closed Interval [a, b]

★★★ 04. Worked Numerical Example · Professor-Followed Book · Howard Anton Sec. 2.2 & Sec. 4.4 (Differentiability & Absolute Extrema)

  • By the Extreme Value Theorem, evaluate \(f(x)\) at all critical points \(c \in (a, b)\) where \(f'(c) = 0\) or f′(c) DNE, and at the endpoints \(x = a, b\).
Closed-Interval Absolute Extrema Procedure
Max / Min on \([a, b] = \max / \min \{ f(a), f(c_{1}), f(c_{2}), ..., f(b) \}\) where \(f'(c_{i}) = 0\) or DNE
Problem / Theorem Question
  • (1) [Anton Sec. 4.4] Find the absolute maximum and minimum values of \(f(x) = 2x^{3} - 15x^{2} + 36x\) on the closed interval [1, 5].
  • (2) [Anton Sec. 4.4] Find the absolute extrema of \(g(x) = x - 2 \sin x\) on \([0, 2\pi ]\).
1. Polynomial on \([1, 5]: f(x) = 2x^{3} - 15x^{2} + 36x\)

Differentiate \(f(x)\), find critical points in (1, 5), and compare values:

\(f(x) = 2x^{3} - 15x^{2} + 36x\)

\(\implies f'(x) = 6x^{2} - 30x + 36\)

\(= 6(x - 2)(x - 3)\)

Setting \(f'(x) = 0\)

\(\implies x = 2, 3 \in (1, 5)\)

Evaluating at critical points and endpoints:

\(f(1) = 2(1) - 15(1) + 36(1)\)

\(= 23\)

\(f(2) = 2(8) - 15(4) + 36(2)\)

\(= 28\)

\(f(3) = 2(27) - 15(9) + 36(3)\)

\(= 27\)

\(f(5) = 2(125) - 15(25) + 36(5)\)

\(= 55\)

∴ Absolute Maximum \(= 55\) (at \(x = 5\)), Absolute Minimum \(= 23\) (at \(x = 1\))

2. Trigonometric Function on \([0, 2\pi ]: g(x) = x - 2 \sin x\)

Solve \(g'(x) = 1 - 2 \cos x = 0\) on \((0, 2\pi )\):

\(g'(x) = 1 - 2 \cos x\)

\(= 0\)

\(\implies \cos x = \frac{1}{2}\)

\(\implies x = \frac{\pi }{3}, \frac{5\pi }{3}\)

Evaluating \(g(x)\) at \(x = 0, \frac{\pi }{3}, \frac{5\pi }{3}, 2\pi \):

\(g(0) = 0\)

\(g(\frac{\pi }{3}) = \frac{\pi }{3} - \sqrt{3} \approx -0.685\)

\(g(\frac{5\pi }{3}) = \frac{5\pi }{3} + \sqrt{3} \approx 6.968\)

\(g(2\pi ) = 2\pi \approx 6.283\)

∴ Absolute Maximum \(= \frac{5\pi }{3} + \sqrt{3}\) (at \(x = \frac{5\pi }{3}\)), Absolute Minimum \(= \frac{\pi }{3} - \sqrt{3}\) (at \(x = \frac{\pi }{3}\))

Syllabus Book Derivations: n-th Derivative of \(\tan^{-1}(\frac{x}{a}) \&\) Implicit Power-Sum Leibniz Theorem

★ 05. Worked Numerical Example · Syllabus Book · J. Edwards (Differential Calculus, Ch. III) & F. Ayres (Schaum’s Calculus)

  • Using the polar substitution \(x =\) a \(\cot \theta \) reduces \(y = \tan^{-1}(\frac{x}{a})\) and \(y = \frac{1}{x^{2} + a^{2}}\) to compact trigonometric n-th derivatives.
Edwards’ Polar Substitution for \(y = \tan^{-1}(\frac{x}{a})\)
\(y_{n} = [(-1)^{n-1} \frac{(n - 1)!}{a^{n}}] \cdot \sin^{n}\theta \cdot \sin(n\theta )\), where \(\theta = \cot^{-1}(\frac{x}{a})\)
Problem / Theorem Question
  • (1) [\(J\). Edwards, Differential Calculus] If \(y = \tan^{-1}(x)\), prove that \((1 + x^{2})y_{n+2} + 2(n + 1)x y_{n+1} + n(n + 1)y_{n} = 0\) and find \(y_{n}(0)\).
  • (2) [\(J\). Edwards & F. Ayres] If \(y^{\frac{1}{m}} + y^{-\frac{1}{m}} = 2x\), prove that \((x^{2} - 1)y_{n+2} + (2n + 1)x y_{n+1} + (n^{2} - m^{2})y_{n} = 0\).
1. Edwards’ Derivation for \(y = \tan^{-1}(x)\)

Differentiate \(y_{1} = \frac{1}{1 + x^{2}}\) to get \((1 + x^{2})y_{1} = 1\) and apply Leibniz’s Theorem:

Given, \(y = \tan^{-1}(x)\)

\(\implies y_{1} = \frac{1}{1 + x^{2}}\)

\(\implies (1 + x^{2})y_{1} = 1\)

\(\implies (1 + x^{2})y_{2} + 2x y_{1} = 0\)

Applying Leibniz’s Theorem \((D^{n})\):

\([(1 + x^{2})y_{n+2} + 2nx y_{n+1} + n(n - 1)y_{n}] + 2[x y_{n+1} + n y_{n}] = 0\)

∴ \((1 + x^{2})y_{n+2} + 2(n + 1)x y_{n+1} + n(n + 1)y_{n} = 0\)

∴ At \(x = 0: y_{n}(0) = 0\) (even \(n); y_{n}(0) = (-1)^{\frac{n-1}{2}} (n - 1\))! (odd \(n\))

2. Implicit Power-Sum Equation: \(y^{\frac{1}{m}} + y^{-\frac{1}{m}} = 2x\)

Solve the quadratic in \(y^{\frac{1}{m}}\) and eliminate the radical:

Given, \(y^{\frac{1}{m}} + \frac{1}{y^{\frac{1}{m}}} = 2x\)

\(\implies (y^{\frac{1}{m}})^{2} - 2x(y^{\frac{1}{m}}) + 1 = 0\)

\(\implies y^{\frac{1}{m}} = x \pm \sqrt{x^{2} - 1}\)

\(\implies y = (x \pm \sqrt{x^{2} - 1})^{m}\)

\(\implies y_{1} = \pm m \frac{y}{\sqrt{x^{2} - 1}}\)

\(\implies (x^{2} - 1)y_{1}^{2} = m^{2} y^{2}\)

\(\implies (x^{2} - 1)y_{2} + x y_{1} - m^{2} y = 0\)

∴ \((x^{2} - 1)y_{n+2} + (2n + 1)x y_{n+1} + (n^{2} - m^{2})y_{n} = 0\)

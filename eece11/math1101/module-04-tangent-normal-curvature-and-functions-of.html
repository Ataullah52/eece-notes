Problem / Theorem Question
  • [Class Lecture & PUST 2020–21 Q3a] State and prove Euler’s Theorem on homogeneous functions of degree \(n\) in two variables \(x\) and \(y\) for both (i) First-Order Partial Derivatives and (ii) Second-Order Partial Derivatives.
Part (i) — First-Order Proof of Euler’s Theorem

Write \(u = x^{n} \varphi (\frac{y}{x})\) and differentiate partially with respect to \(x\) and \(y\):

Since \(u(x, y)\) is homogeneous of degree \(n\), let:

\(u = x^{n} \varphi (\frac{y}{x})\)

Differentiating partially w.r.t. \(x\) and \(y\):

\(\frac{\partial u}{\partial x} = n x^{n-1} \varphi (\frac{y}{x}) - x^{n-2} y \varphi '(\frac{y}{x})\)

\(\frac{\partial u}{\partial y} = x^{n-1} \varphi '(\frac{y}{x})\)

Multiplying by \(x\) and \(y\) respectively and adding:

\(x(\frac{\partial u}{\partial x}) + y(\frac{\partial u}{\partial y}) = n x^{n} \varphi (\frac{y}{x})\)

∴ \(x(\frac{\partial u}{\partial x}) + y(\frac{\partial u}{\partial y}) = n u\)

Part (ii) — Second-Order Extension Proof

Differentiate the first-order Euler identity partially with respect to \(x\) and \(y\):

Differentiating \(x u_{x} + y u_{y} = n u\) w.r.t. \(x\) and \(y\):

\(x u_{xx} + y u_{xy} = (n - 1) u_{x} --- (1)\)

\(x u_{xy} + y u_{yy} = (n - 1) u_{y} --- (2)\)

Multiplying (1) by \(x\) and (2) by \(y\) and adding:

\(x^{2} u_{xx} + 2xy u_{xy} + y^{2} u_{yy} = (n - 1)(x u_{x} + y u_{y})\)

∴ \(x^{2}(\frac{\partial ^{2}u}{\partial x^{2}}) + 2xy(\frac{\partial ^{2}u}{\partial x\partial y}) + y^{2}(\frac{\partial ^{2}u}{\partial y^{2}}) = n(n - 1)u\)

Problem / Theorem Question

  • (2) [PUST 2022–23 Q3b & 2020–21 Q3c] Find the asymptotes of \(x^{3} + y^{3} - 3axy = 0\) and \(y^{3} - x^{2}y - 2xy^{2} + 2x^{3} - 7xy + 3y^{2} + 2x^{2} + 2x + 2y + 1 = 0\).

1. PUST 2020–21 Q4(c): Tangent & Normal to \(x^{2} +\) xy \(+ y^{2} = 7\) at (1, 2)

Differentiate implicitly with respect to \(x\):

\(2x + (x \frac{dy}{dx} + y) + 2y \frac{dy}{dx} = 0\)

\(\implies \frac{dy}{dx} = -\frac{2x + y}{x + 2y}\)

At \((1, 2): m = -\frac{2(1) + 2}{1 + 2(2)}\)

\(= -\frac{4}{5}\)

• Tangent: \(y - 2 = (-\frac{4}{5})(x - 1)\)

• Normal: \(y - 2 = (+\frac{5}{4})(x - 1)\)

∴ Tangent: \(4x + 5y - 14 = 0\), Normal: \(5x - 4y + 3 = 0\)

2. PUST 2022–23 Q3(b) & 2020–21 Q3(c): Oblique Asymptotes of Cubic Curves

Put \(x = 1, y = m\) in degree-3 and degree-2 homogeneous parts:

(a) PUST 2022–23 Q3\((b): x^{3} + y^{3} - 3axy = 0\)

\(\varphi _{3}(m) = 1 + m^{3}\)

\(= 0\)

\(\implies m = -1\)

\(c = -\frac{\varphi _{2}(m)}{\varphi '_{3}(m)}\)

\(= -\frac{-3am}{3m^{2}}\)

\(= \frac{a}{m}\)

\(= -a\)

∴ Asymptote: \(x + y + a = 0\)

(b) PUST 2020–21 Q3\((c): y^{3} - x^{2}y - 2xy^{2} + 2x^{3} - 7xy + 3y^{2} + 2x^{2} + 2x + 2y + 1 = 0\)

\(\varphi _{3}(m) = m^{3} - 2m^{2} - m + 2\)

\(= (m - 1)(m + 1)(m - 2)\)

\(= 0\)

\(\implies m = 1, -1, 2\)

\(c = -\frac{3m^{2} - 7m + 2}{3m^{2} - 4m - 1}\)

\(\implies c = -1, -2, 0\)

∴ Asymptotes: \(x - y - 1 = 0, x + y + 2 = 0, 2x - y = 0\)

3. PUST 2022–23 Q3(c), 2020–21 Q4(b) & 2021–22 Q3(b): Radius of Curvature \(\rho \)

Vertical step-by-step curvature derivations:

(a) PUST 2022–23 Q3\((c): \sqrt{x} + \sqrt{y} = \sqrt{ }\)a at \((\frac{a}{4}, \frac{a}{4})\)

\(y_{1} = -\frac{\sqrt{y}}{\sqrt{x}}\)

\(= -1, y_{2} = \frac{\sqrt{a}}{2(\frac{a}{4})^{\frac{3}{2}}}\)

\(= \frac{4}{a}\)

\(\rho = (1 + 1)\frac{^{\frac{3}{2}}}{\frac{4}{a}}\)

∴ \(\rho = \frac{a}{\sqrt{2}}\)

(b) PUST 2020–21 Q4\((b): x^{\frac{2}{3}} + y^{\frac{2}{3}} = a^{\frac{2}{3}}\)

\(y_{1} = -(\frac{y}{x})^{\frac{1}{3}}, 1 + y_{1}^{2} = \frac{a^{\frac{2}{3}}}{x^{\frac{2}{3}}}, y_{2} = \frac{a^{\frac{2}{3}}}{3 x^{\frac{4}{3}} y^{\frac{1}{3}}}\)

∴ \(\rho = 3\)(axy)\(^{\frac{1}{3}}\)

(c) PUST 2021–22 Q3\((b): xy^{2} = a^{3} - x^{3}\) at (a, 0)

Shift origin to (a, 0) via \(x = a + X, y = Y\):

\((a + X)Y^{2} = -3a^{2}X - 3aX^{2} - X^{3}\) [Tangent at new origin is \(X = 0\)]

\(\rho = \lim\limits_{Y\to 0} |\frac{Y^{2}}{2X}|\)

∴ \(\rho = \frac{3a}{2}\)

★★★★★ 03. Previous Year Question (PYQ) · Previous Year Question · PUST EECE 2022–23 Q3(a) [5 Marks], 2021–22 Q3(a) [4.5 Marks], Q4(c) [4.5 Marks], 2020–21 Q3(b) [4.5 Marks] · Asked in: 2022–23, 2021–22, 2020–21 (4.5–5 Marks)

Problem / Theorem Question
  • (1) [PUST 2021–22 Q3a] If \(u = \sin^{-1}[\frac{x^{2} + y^{2}}{x + y}]\), show that \(x \frac{\partial u}{\partial x} + y \frac{\partial u}{\partial y} = \tan u\).
  • (2) [PUST 2022–23 Q3a] If \(u = \tan^{-1}[\frac{x^{3} + y^{3}}{x - y}]\), prove that \(x \frac{\partial u}{\partial x} + y \frac{\partial u}{\partial y} = \sin 2u\).
  • (3) [PUST 2020–21 Q3b] If \(u = \cos^{-1}[\frac{x + y}{\sqrt{x} + \sqrt{y}}]\), show that \(x \frac{\partial u}{\partial x} + y \frac{\partial u}{\partial y} + (\frac{1}{2}) \cot u = 0\).
  • (4) [PUST 2021–22 Q4c] If \(u = x^{2} - 2y^{2}\) and \(v = 2x^{2} - y^{2}\), where \(x = r \cos \theta , y = r \sin \theta \), show that \(\frac{\partial (u, v)}{\partial (r, \theta )} = 6r^{3} \sin 2\theta \).
1. PUST 2021–22 Q3(a), 2022–23 Q3(a) & 2020–21 Q3(b): Euler’s Theorem Problems

Rewrite as \(z =\) trig(u) so \(z\) is homogeneous of degree \(n\), then apply \(x z_{x} + y z_{y} = n z\):

(a) PUST 2021–22 Q3\((a): z = \sin u\)

\(= \frac{x^{2} + y^{2}}{x + y}\) [Homogeneous of degree \(n = 1\)]

\(x \frac{\partial z}{\partial x} + y \frac{\partial z}{\partial y} = 1 \cdot z\)

\(\implies \cos u (x \frac{\partial u}{\partial x} + y \frac{\partial u}{\partial y}) = \sin u\)

∴ \(x(\frac{\partial u}{\partial x}) + y(\frac{\partial u}{\partial y}) = \tan u\)

(b) PUST 2022–23 Q3\((a): z = \tan u\)

\(= \frac{x^{3} + y^{3}}{x - y}\) [Homogeneous of degree \(n = 2\)]

\(x \frac{\partial z}{\partial x} + y \frac{\partial z}{\partial y} = 2z\)

\(\implies \sec^{2}u (x \frac{\partial u}{\partial x} + y \frac{\partial u}{\partial y}) = 2 \tan u\)

∴ \(x(\frac{\partial u}{\partial x}) + y(\frac{\partial u}{\partial y}) = \sin 2u\)

(c) PUST 2020–21 Q3\((b): z = \cos u\)

\(= \frac{x + y}{\sqrt{x} + \sqrt{y}}\) [Homogeneous of degree \(n = \frac{1}{2}\)]

\(-\sin u (x \frac{\partial u}{\partial x} + y \frac{\partial u}{\partial y}) = (\frac{1}{2}) \cos u\)

∴ \(x(\frac{\partial u}{\partial x}) + y(\frac{\partial u}{\partial y}) + (\frac{1}{2}) \cot u = 0\)

2. PUST 2021–22 Q4(c): Jacobian Chain Rule Derivation

Compute \(\frac{\partial (u, v)}{\partial (x, y)}\) and polar Jacobian \(\frac{\partial (x, y)}{\partial (r, \theta )} = r\):

\(\frac{\partial (u, v)}{\partial (x, y)} = (2x)(-2y) - (-4y)(4x)\)

\(= -4xy + 16xy\)

\(= 12xy\)

\(\frac{\partial (x, y)}{\partial (r, \theta )} = r \cos^{2}\theta + r \sin^{2}\theta \)

\(= r\)

By the Jacobian Chain Rule:

\(\frac{\partial (u, v)}{\partial (r, \theta )} = [\frac{\partial (u, v)}{\partial (x, y)}] \cdot [\frac{\partial (x, y)}{\partial (r, \theta )}]\)

\(= (12 r \cos \theta \cdot r \sin \theta ) \cdot r\)

∴ \(\frac{\partial (u, v)}{\partial (r, \theta )} = 6r^{3} \sin 2\theta \)

Problem / Theorem Question
  • (1) [Anton 13.2] Show that \(\lim\limits_{(x,y)\to (0,0)}\) (xy) \(/ (x^{2} + y^{2})\) does not exist.
  • (2) [Anton 13.8] Find and classify the stationary points of \(f(x, y) = x^{3} + y^{3} - 3axy (a > 0)\) using the Hessian determinant.
1. Two-Variable Path Test: \(\lim\limits_{(x,y)\to (0,0)}\) (xy) \(/ (x^{2} + y^{2})\)

Approach (0, 0) along the family of straight lines \(y =\) mx:

Along path \(y =\) mx:

\(\lim\limits_{x\to 0} (x \cdot \) mx) \(/ (x^{2} + m^{2}x^{2}) = \lim\limits_{x\to 0} \frac{m}{1 + m^{2}}\)

\(= \frac{m}{1 + m^{2}}\)

• For \(m = 0\) (along x-axis): Limit \(= 0\)

• For \(m = 1\) (along \(y = x\)): Limit \(= \frac{1}{2}\)

∴ Since the limit depends on the slope \(m, \lim\limits_{(x,y)\to (0,0)}\) (xy) \(/ (x^{2} + y^{2})\) does not exist.

2. Worked Hessian Example: Extrema of \(f(x, y) = x^{3} + y^{3} - 3axy (a > 0)\)

Solve \(f_{x} = 0, f_{y} = 0\) for stationary points and evaluate \(H(x, y) = f_{xx}f_{yy} - f_{xy}^{2}\):

\(f_{x} = 3x^{2} - 3ay\)

\(= 0\)

\(\implies y = \frac{x^{2}}{a}\)

\(f_{y} = 3y^{2} - 3ax\)

\(= 0\)

\(\implies x(x^{3} - a^{3}) = 0\)

\(\implies \) Stationary points: (0, 0) and (a, a)

Second-order partials: \(f_{xx} = 6x, f_{yy} = 6y, f_{xy} = -3a\)

Hessian: \(H(x, y) = 36xy - 9a^{2}\)

• At \((0, 0): H(0, 0) = -9a^{2} < 0 \implies \) Saddle Point

• At \((a, a): H(a, a) = 27a^{2} > 0\) and \(f_{xx}(a, a) = 6a > 0 \implies \) Local Minimum

∴ (0, 0) is a Saddle Point; (a, a) gives Local Minimum \(f_{\min} = -a^{3}\)

Syllabus Book Problems: Polar Radius of Curvature of Cardioid & Inverse Jacobian Identity \(J \cdot J' = 1\)

★ 05. Worked Numerical Example · Syllabus Book · J. Edwards (Differential Calculus) & M. R. Spiegel (Advanced Calculus)

  • For polar curve \(r = f(\theta )\), radius of curvature is \(\rho = (r^{2} + r_{1}^{2})^{\frac{3}{2}} / |r^{2} + 2r_{1}^{2} - r r_{2}|\).
Inverse Jacobian Product Identity & Implicit Derivative Formula
\([\frac{\partial (x, y)}{\partial (r, \theta )}] \cdot [\frac{\partial (r, \theta )}{\partial (x, y)}] = 1\)\(F(x, y) = 0 \implies \frac{dy}{dx} = - \frac{\frac{\partial F}{\partial x}}{\frac{\partial F}{\partial y}}\)
Problem / Theorem Question
  • (1) [\(J\). Edwards] Find the radius of curvature at any point \((r, \theta )\) on the cardioid \(r = a(1 + \cos \theta )\).
  • (2) [M. R. Spiegel] Verify that \(J \cdot J' = 1\) for the transformation \(x = r \cos \theta , y = r \sin \theta \).
1. Polar Radius of Curvature of Cardioid \(r = a(1 + \cos \theta )\)

Compute \(r_{1} = \frac{dr}{d\theta }\) and \(r_{2} = \frac{d^{2}r}{d\theta ^{2}}\) and substitute into the polar \(\rho \) formula:

Given, \(r = a(1 + \cos \theta )\)

\(r_{1} = -\)a \(\sin \theta , r_{2} = -\)a \(\cos \theta \)

\(r^{2} + r_{1}^{2} = a^{2}(1 + \cos \theta )^{2} + a^{2} \sin^{2}\theta \)

\(= 2a^{2}(1 + \cos \theta )\)

\(= 4a^{2} \cos^{2}(\frac{\theta }{2})\)

\(r^{2} + 2r_{1}^{2} - r r_{2} = 3a^{2}(1 + \cos \theta )\)

\(= 6a^{2} \cos^{2}(\frac{\theta }{2})\)

Substituting into \(\rho = (r^{2} + r_{1}^{2})^{\frac{3}{2}} / |r^{2} + 2r_{1}^{2} - r r_{2}|\):

\(\rho = \frac{8a^{3} \cos^{3}(\frac{\theta }{2})}{6a^{2} \cos^{2}(\frac{\theta }{2})}\)

∴ \(\rho = (\frac{4a}{3}) \cos(\frac{\theta }{2}) = (\frac{2}{3})\sqrt{2ar}\)

2. Verification of Inverse Jacobian Identity \(J \cdot J' = 1\)

Compute \(J = \frac{\partial (x,y)}{\partial (r,\theta )}\) and \(J' = \frac{\partial (r,\theta )}{\partial (x,y)}\) from \(r^{2} = x^{2} + y^{2}, \theta = \tan^{-1}(\frac{y}{x})\):

\(J = \frac{\partial (x, y)}{\partial (r, \theta )}\)

\(= r \cos^{2}\theta + r \sin^{2}\theta \)

\(= r\)

From \(r = \sqrt{x^{2} + y^{2}}\) and \(\theta = \tan^{-1}(\frac{y}{x})\):

\(r_{x} = \frac{x}{r}\)

\(= \cos \theta , r_{y} = \frac{y}{r}\)

\(= \sin \theta \)

\(\theta _{x} = -\frac{y}{r^{2}}\)

\(= -\frac{\sin \theta }{r}, \theta _{y} = \frac{x}{r^{2}}\)

\(= \frac{\cos \theta }{r}\)

\(J' = \frac{\partial (r, \theta )}{\partial (x, y)}\)

\(= \frac{\cos^{2}\theta }{r} + \frac{\sin^{2}\theta }{r}\)

\(= \frac{1}{r}\)

∴ \(J \cdot J' = r \cdot (\frac{1}{r}) = 1\)

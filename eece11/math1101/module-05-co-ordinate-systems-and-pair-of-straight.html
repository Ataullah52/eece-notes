PUST Past Exam Questions on Transformation of Coordinates (Removing First-Degree & XY Terms)

P2 · Previous Year Question + P1 · Previous Year Question · Previous Year Question · PUST EECE 2022–23 & 2020–21 Semester Final Exams [7 Marks] · Asked in: 2022–23, 2020–21 (7 Marks)

Complete vertical exam solutions for removing linear terms via center translation and removing the xy-term via orthogonal invariants.

Problem / Theorem Question

(1) [PUST Past Exam — 7 Marks] Transform the equation \(17x^{2} + 18xy - 7y^{2} - 16x - 32y - 18 = 0\) to one in which there are no first-degree terms and no xy-term.

(2) [PUST Past Exam — 5 Marks] Transform \(x^{2} + 4xy + y^{2} - 2x + 2y - 6 = 0\) to standard form without linear or xy-terms.

Step-by-step solution
1. PUST Exam Problem: \(17x^{2} + 18xy - 7y^{2} - 16x - 32y - 18 = 0\)

Compare with \(ax^{2} + 2hxy + by^{2} + 2gx + 2fy + c = 0: a = 17, h = 9, b = -7, g = -8, f = -16, c = -18\):

Step 1 (Translation of Origin to \((\alpha , \beta )\) to Remove First-Degree Terms):

\(17\alpha + 9\beta - 8 = 0\)

\(9\alpha - 7\beta - 16 = 0\)

\(\implies \alpha = 1, \beta = -1\)

New constant term:

\(c' = g\alpha + f\beta + c\)

\(= (-8)(1) + (-16)(-1) - 18\)

\(= -10\)

Translated equation: \(17X^{2} + 18XY - 7Y^{2} - 10 = 0\)

Step 2 (Rotation of Axes to Remove the XY-Term):

\(a' + b' = a + b\)

\(= 17 - 7\)

\(= 10\)

\(a'b' =\) ab \(- h^{2}\)

\(= (17)(-7) - 9^{2}\)

\(= -200\)

Characteristic quadratic:

\(\lambda ^{2} - 10\lambda - 200 = 0\)

\(\implies (\lambda - 20)(\lambda + 10) = 0\)

\(\implies a' = 20, b' = -10\)

Substituting into \(a'x'^{2} + b'y'^{2} + c' = 0\):

\(20x'^{2} - 10y'^{2} - 10 = 0\)

∴ \(2x'^{2} - y'^{2} = 1\)

2. PUST Exam Problem: \(x^{2} + 4xy + y^{2} - 2x + 2y - 6 = 0\)

Here \(a = 1, h = 2, b = 1, g = -1, f = 1, c = -6\):

Center equations:

\(\alpha + 2\beta - 1 = 0\) and \(2\alpha + \beta + 1 = 0\)

\(\implies \alpha = -1, \beta = 1\)

New constant:

\(c' = g\alpha + f\beta + c\)

\(= (-1)(-1) + (1)(1) - 6\)

\(= -4\)

Characteristic equation:

\(\lambda ^{2} - 2\lambda - 3 = 0\)

\(\implies \lambda = 3, -1\)

∴ \(3x'^{2} - y'^{2} = 4\)

PUST Past Exam Questions on General Pair of Straight Lines \((\Delta = 0) \&\) Homogenization

P2 · Previous Year Question + P1 · Previous Year Question · Previous Year Question · PUST EECE 2022–23, 2021–22 & 2020–21 Semester Final Exams [5 Marks] · Asked in: 2022–23, 2021–22, 2020–21 (5 Marks)

Verify \(\Delta = 0\), factor into separate linear equations, find intersection and angle \(\theta \), and homogenize curve-line intersections.

Problem / Theorem Question

(1) [PUST Past Exam — 5 Marks] Prove that \(2x^{2} + 7xy + 3y^{2} + 8x + 14y + 8 = 0\) represents a pair of straight lines. Find their separate equations, point of intersection, and angle between them.

(2) [PUST Past Exam — 5 Marks] Show that the straight lines joining the origin to the points of intersection of the circle \(x^{2} + y^{2} = a^{2}\) and the line lx + my \(= 1\) are at right angles if \(a^{2}(l^{2} + m^{2}) = 2\).

Step-by-step solution
1. General Pair of Straight Lines: \(2x^{2} + 7xy + 3y^{2} + 8x + 14y + 8 = 0\)

Identify \(a = 2, h = \frac{7}{2}, b = 3, g = 4, f = 7, c = 8\) and verify \(\Delta = 0\):

Step 1 (Testing \(\Delta = 0\)):

\(\Delta =\) abc \(+ 2fgh - af^{2} - bg^{2} - ch^{2}\)

\(= 48 + 196 - 98 - 48 - 98\)

\(= 0\)

Step 2 (Separate Equations & Point of Intersection):

\(2x^{2} + 7xy + 3y^{2} + 8x + 14y + 8 \equiv (2x + y + 4)(x + 3y + 2) = 0\)

Solving \(2x + y + 4 = 0\) and \(x + 3y + 2 = 0\) gives (−2, 0).

∴ Lines: \(2x + y + 4 = 0\) and \(x + 3y + 2 = 0\); Intersection: (−2, 0)

Step 3 (Angle \(\theta \) Between the Lines):

\(\tan \theta = 2\sqrt{ }(h^{2} -\) ab) \(/ |a + b|\)

\(= \frac{2 \cdot (\frac{5}{2})}{2 + 3}\)

\(= 1\)

∴ \(\theta = 45^{\circ}\)

2. Homogenization Proof: Circle \(x^{2} + y^{2} = a^{2}\) and Line lx + my \(= 1\)

Homogenize \(x^{2} + y^{2} = a^{2}(1)^{2}\) using lx + my \(= 1\) and enforce \(a + b = 0\):

Homogenizing \(x^{2} + y^{2} = a^{2}\) with lx + my

\(= 1\):

\(x^{2} + y^{2} = a^{2}\)(lx + my)\(^{2}\)

\(\implies (1 - a^{2}l^{2})x^{2} - 2a^{2}\)lm xy \(+ (1 - a^{2}m^{2})y^{2} = 0\)

For perpendicular lines through the origin, (coeff. of \(x^{2}) +\) (coeff. of \(y^{2}) = 0\):

\((1 - a^{2}l^{2}) + (1 - a^{2}m^{2}) = 0\)

∴ \(a^{2}(l^{2} + m^{2}) = 2\)

Rahman & Bhattacharjee Worked Problems: Angle Bisectors & Perpendicular Line Pairs

P3 · Professor-Followed Book · Worked Numerical Example · Professor-Followed Book · Rahman & Bhattacharjee (Co-ordinate Geometry, Ch. 2)

The pair of lines through the origin perpendicular to \(ax^{2} + 2hxy + by^{2} = 0\) is \(bx^{2} - 2hxy + ay^{2} = 0\), and the angle bisectors \(\frac{x^{2} - y^{2}}{a - b} =\) xy/h are always orthogonal.

Problem / Theorem Question

(1) [Rahman & Bhattacharjee] Prove that the bisectors of the angles between the lines \(ax^{2} + 2hxy + by^{2} = 0\) are given by \(\frac{x^{2} - y^{2}}{a - b} =\) xy/h and are always mutually perpendicular.

(2) Find the equation of the pair of straight lines through the origin perpendicular to \(5x^{2} - 8xy + 3y^{2} = 0\).

Step-by-step solution
1. Equation & Orthogonality of Angle Bisectors

Let \(ax^{2} + 2hxy + by^{2} \equiv b(y - m_{1}x)(y - m_{2}x)\) with \(m_{1} + m_{2} = -\frac{2h}{b}, m_{1}m_{2} = \frac{a}{b}\):

Equating perpendicular distances to \(y - m_{1}x = 0\) and \(y - m_{2}x = 0\) gives:

\(h(x^{2} - y^{2}) - (a - b)\)xy \(= 0\)

\(\iff \frac{x^{2} - y^{2}}{a - b} =\) xy / h

Here (coeff. of \(x^{2}) +\) (coeff. of \(y^{2}) = h + (-h\))

\(= 0\).

∴ Bisectors: \(\frac{x^{2} - y^{2}}{a - b} =\) xy / h (Always mutually perpendicular)

2. Pair of Lines Through Origin Perpendicular to \(5x^{2} - 8xy + 3y^{2} = 0\)

Interchange a and \(b\) and reverse the sign of \(2h (bx^{2} - 2hxy + ay^{2} = 0)\):

Given pair: \(5x^{2} - 8xy + 3y^{2} = 0 (a = 5, 2h = -8, b = 3)\)

Replacing (a, 2h, b) by (b, −2h, a):

∴ \(3x^{2} + 8xy + 5y^{2} = 0\)

Syllabus Book Problems (Askwith): Area of Triangle Formed by \(ax^{2} + 2hxy + by^{2} = 0\) and lx + my \(+ n = 0\)

P4 · Syllabus Book · Worked Numerical Example · Syllabus Book · E. H. Askwith (Analytic Geometry of Conic Sections, Ch. III)

The area of the triangle formed by the pair of lines \(ax^{2} + 2hxy + by^{2} = 0\) and the line lx + my \(+ n = 0\) is \(\Delta = n^{2}\sqrt{ }(h^{2} -\) ab) \(/ |am^{2} - 2hlm + bl^{2}|\).

Askwith’s Triangle Area & Parallel Line-Pair Distance Formulas
Area \(= n^{2}\sqrt{ }(h^{2} -\) ab) \(/ |am^{2} - 2hlm + bl^{2}| |\) Parallel Distance (\(h^{2} =\) ab): \(d = 2\sqrt{ }[(g^{2} -\) ac) \(/ (a(a + b))\)]
Problem / Theorem Question

(1) [E. H. Askwith] Find the area of the triangle formed by the lines \(x^{2} - 4xy + y^{2} = 0\) and the line \(x + y - 2 = 0\).

(2) Find the distance between the parallel straight lines \(9x^{2} - 24xy + 16y^{2} - 12x + 16y - 12 = 0\).

Step-by-step solution
1. Area of Triangle Formed by \(x^{2} - 4xy + y^{2} = 0\) and \(x + y - 2 = 0\)

Here \(a = 1, h = -2, b = 1\) and \(l = 1, m = 1, n = -2\):

\(\sqrt{ }(h^{2} -\) ab) \(= \sqrt{4 - 1}\)

\(= \sqrt{3}\)

\(am^{2} - 2hlm + bl^{2} = 1(1)^{2} - 2(-2)(1)(1) + 1(1)^{2}\)

\(= 6\)

Area \(= n^{2} \sqrt{ }(h^{2} -\) ab) \(/ |am^{2} - 2hlm + bl^{2}|\)

\(= (-2)^{2} \frac{\sqrt{3}}{6}\)

∴ Area \(= \frac{2\sqrt{3}}{3} = \frac{2}{\sqrt{3}}\) sq. units

2. Distance Between Parallel Pair \(9x^{2} - 24xy + 16y^{2} - 12x + 16y - 12 = 0\)

Factor as a quadratic in \((3x - 4y)\) and apply the parallel line distance formula:

\((3x - 4y)^{2} - 4(3x - 4y) - 12 = 0\)

\(\implies (3x - 4y - 6)(3x - 4y + 2) = 0\)

Distance between \(3x - 4y - 6 = 0\) and \(3x - 4y + 2 = 0\):

\(d = |-6 - \frac{2|}{\sqrt{3^{2} + (-4)^{2}}}\)

∴ \(d = \frac{8}{5}\) units

3D Coordinate Conversions (Cartesian, Cylindrical, Spherical), Direction Cosines & Plane/Line Formulas

★★★★★ 01. Definition, Law & Formula

  • Direction cosines \(l = \cos \alpha , m = \cos \beta , n = \cos \gamma \) satisfy \(l^{2} + m^{2} + n^{2} = 1\).
  • Two lines in 3D are coplanar iff their \(3\times 3\) determinant vanishes.
Direction Cosines Identity & Normalization from Direction Ratios (a, b, c)
\(l^{2} + m^{2} + n^{2} = 1\)\(l = \pm \frac{a}{\sqrt{a^{2} + b^{2} + c^{2}}}, m = \pm \frac{b}{\sqrt{a^{2} + b^{2} + c^{2}}}, n = \pm \frac{c}{\sqrt{a^{2} + b^{2} + c^{2}}}\)
Projection of Segment PQ on Line (l, m, n) & Angle \(\theta \) Between Two Lines
Proj \(= l(x_{2} - x_{1}) + m(y_{2} - y_{1}) + n(z_{2} - z_{1})\)\(\cos \theta = l_{1}l_{2} + m_{1}m_{2} + n_{1}n_{2}\)
Equations of a Plane & Perpendicular Distance from \((x_{1}, y_{1}, z_{1})\)
Intercept Form: \(\frac{x}{a} + \frac{y}{b} + \frac{z}{c} = 1\)Intersection: \(P_{1} + \lambda P_{2} = 0\)\(d = |Ax_{1} + By_{1} + Cz_{1} + \frac{D|}{\sqrt{A^{2} + B^{2} + C^{2}}}\)
Symmetric Line Equation & Coplanarity Determinant
\(\frac{x - x_{1}}{l} = \frac{y - y_{1}}{m} = \frac{z - z_{1}}{n} = r\)Coplanar iff \(\det[[x_{2}-x_{1}, y_{2}-y_{1}, z_{2}-z_{1}], [l_{1}, m_{1}, n_{1}], [l_{2}, m_{2}, n_{2}]] = 0\)

ConversionForward EquationsInverse Equations & Restrictions
Cylindrical \((r, \theta , z) ↔\) Cartesian (x, y, z)\(x = r \cos \theta , y = r \sin \theta , z = z\)\(r^{2} = x^{2} + y^{2}, \tan \theta = \frac{y}{x}, z = z (r \ge 0, 0 \le \theta < 2\pi )\)

PUST Past Exam Questions on Cube Diagonal Theorems & Direction Cosine Algebraic Relations

★★★★★ 02. Previous Year Question (PYQ) · Previous Year Question · PUST EECE 2022–23, 2021–22 & 2020–21 Semester Final Exams [5 Marks] · Asked in: 2022–23, 2021–22, 2020–21 (5 Marks)

  • Angle between any two diagonals of a cube is \(\cos^{-1}(\frac{1}{3})\), and a line making angles \(\alpha , \beta , \gamma , \delta \) with the four diagonals satisfies \(\cos^{2}\alpha + \cos^{2}\beta + \cos^{2}\gamma + \cos^{2}\delta = \frac{4}{3}\).
Problem / Theorem Question
  • (1) [PUST Past Exam — 5 Marks] (a) Find the angle between any two diagonals of a cube.
  • (b) Prove that if a line makes angles \(\alpha , \beta , \gamma , \delta \) with the four diagonals of a cube, then \(\cos^{2}\alpha + \cos^{2}\beta + \cos^{2}\gamma + \cos^{2}\delta = \frac{4}{3}\).
  • (2) [PUST Past Exam — 5 Marks] Find the angle between the two lines whose direction cosines satisfy \(l + m + n = 0\) and \(l^{2} + m^{2} - n^{2} = 0\).
Step-by-step solution
1. Cube Diagonal Theorems (Part a & Part \(b\))

Take a cube of side a with edges along the coordinate axes; the four diagonals have \(DCs (\pm \frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}, \frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}, \frac{1}{\sqrt{3}})\):

Part (a) — Angle between any two diagonals:

\(\cos \theta = |-\frac{1}{3} + \frac{1}{3} + \frac{1}{3}|\)

\(= \frac{1}{3}\)

∴ \(\theta = \cos^{-1}(\frac{1}{3})\)

Part (b) — Sum of squares of cosines with a line of DCs (l, m, n):

\(\cos \alpha = \frac{l + m + n}{\sqrt{3}}, \cos \beta = \frac{-l + m + n}{\sqrt{3}}\),

\(\cos \gamma = \frac{l - m + n}{\sqrt{3}}, \cos \delta = \frac{l + m - n}{\sqrt{3}}\)

Squaring and adding:

\(\cos^{2}\alpha + \cos^{2}\beta + \cos^{2}\gamma + \cos^{2}\delta = \frac{4(l^{2} + m^{2} + n^{2})}{3}\)

∴ \(\cos^{2}\alpha + \cos^{2}\beta + \cos^{2}\gamma + \cos^{2}\delta = \frac{4}{3}\)

2. Algebraic Direction Cosine Relations: \(l + m + n = 0\) and \(l^{2} + m^{2} - n^{2} = 0\)

Eliminate \(n = -(l + m)\) to find the direction ratios of the two lines:

Substituting \(n = -(l + m)\) into \(l^{2} + m^{2} - n^{2}\)

\(= 0\):

\(l^{2} + m^{2} - (l + m)^{2} = 0\)

\(\implies -2lm = 0\)

\(\implies l = 0\) or \(m = 0\)

• When \(l = 0: DRs\) of Line 1: (0, 1, −1)

• When \(m = 0: DRs\) of Line 2: (1, 0, −1)

\(\cos \theta = \frac{(0)(1) + (1)(0) + (-1)(-1)}{\sqrt{2} \cdot \sqrt{2}}\)

\(= \frac{1}{2}\)

∴ \(\theta = 60^{\circ} = \frac{\pi }{3}\)

PUST Past Exam Questions on Plane Families, Centroid Locus & Shortest Distance Between Skew Lines

★★★★★ 03. Previous Year Question (PYQ) · Previous Year Question · PUST EECE 2022–23, 2021–22 & 2020–21 Semester Final Exams [5–7 Marks] · Asked in: 2022–23, 2021–22, 2020–21 (5–7 Marks)

  • Complete exam solutions for plane families \(P_{1} + \lambda P_{2} = 0\), centroid locus \(x^{-2} + y^{-2} + z^{-2} = 9p^{-2}\), and length & equation of the line of shortest distance.
Problem / Theorem Question
  • (1) [PUST Past Exam — 5 Marks] Find the plane through the intersection of \(x + 2y + 3z - 4 = 0\) and \(2x + y - z + 5 = 0\) perpendicular to \(5x + 3y + 6z + 8 = 0\).
  • (2) [PUST Past Exam — 5 Marks] A variable plane at constant distance \(p\) from the origin meets the axes at A, B, C. Show that the locus of the centroid of \(\triangle \)ABC is \(x^{-2} + y^{-2} + z^{-2} = 9p^{-2}\).
  • (3) [PUST Past Exam — 7 Marks] Find the length and equations of the line of shortest distance between \(\frac{x - 3}{3} = \frac{y - 8}{-1} = \frac{z - 3}{1}\) and \(\frac{x + 3}{-3} = \frac{y + 7}{2} = \frac{z - 6}{4}\).
Step-by-step solution
1. Plane Through Intersection & Centroid Locus Proof

Vertical solutions for Plane Problems (1) and (2):

Problem 1 (Intersection Plane):

\((x + 2y + 3z - 4) + \lambda (2x + y - z + 5) = 0\)

Perpendicular to \(5x + 3y + 6z + 8 = 0\):

\(5(1 + 2\lambda ) + 3(2 + \lambda ) + 6(3 - \lambda ) = 0\)

\(\implies \lambda = -\frac{29}{7}\)

∴ \(51x + 15y - 50z + 173 = 0\)

Problem 2 (Centroid Locus):

Plane \(\frac{x}{a} + \frac{y}{b} + \frac{z}{c} = 1\) has centroid (x, y, z)

\(= (\frac{a}{3}, \frac{b}{3}, \frac{c}{3})\)

Since \(a^{-2} + b^{-2} + c^{-2} = p^{-2}\):

\((3x)^{-2} + (3y)^{-2} + (3z)^{-2} = p^{-2}\)

∴ \(x^{-2} + y^{-2} + z^{-2} = 9p^{-2}\)

2. Shortest Distance & Equations of S.D. Line (7 Marks)

Take general points \(P(r_{1})\) on Line 1 and \(Q(r_{2})\) on Line 2 and enforce \(PQ \perp \) Line 1 and \(PQ \perp \) Line 2:

Let \(P(3r_{1} + 3, -r_{1} + 8, r_{1} + 3)\) and \(Q(-3r_{2} - 3, 2r_{2} - 7, 4r_{2} + 6)\).

Enforcing \(PQ \perp (3, -1, 1)\) and \(PQ \perp (-3, 2, 4)\):

\(-11r_{1} - 7r_{2} = 0\) and \(7r_{1} + 29r_{2} = 0\)

\(\implies r_{1} = 0, r_{2} = 0\)

Feet of perpendicular: \(P = (3, 8, 3), Q = (-3, -7, 6), DRs\) of \(PQ = (-6, -15, 3) \propto (2, 5, -1)\)

∴ Shortest Distance, \(S.D. = 3\sqrt{30}\)

∴ S.D. Line: \(\frac{x - 3}{2} = \frac{y - 8}{5} = \frac{z - 3}{-1}\)

Rahman & Bhattacharjee: Unsymmetric-to-Symmetric Line Conversion & Coplanar Lines Plane Equation

★★★ 04. Worked Numerical Example · Professor-Followed Book · Rahman & Bhattacharjee (3D Geometry, Ch. 3) & Anton Ch. 11

  • Find the direction ratios (l, m, n) of the intersection of two planes by taking the cross product of their normal vectors, and find a point by setting \(z = 0\).
Problem / Theorem Question
  • (1) [Rahman & Bhattacharjee] Reduce the unsymmetric line \(x + y + z + 1 = 0 = 4x + y - 2z + 2\) to symmetric form.
  • (2) Show that the lines \(\frac{x + 1}{-3} = \frac{y - 3}{2} = \frac{z + 2}{1}\) and \(\frac{x}{1} = \frac{y - 7}{-3} = \frac{z + 7}{2}\) are coplanar, and find the equation of the plane containing them.
Step-by-step solution
1. Unsymmetric to Symmetric Form: \(x + y + z + 1 = 0, 4x + y - 2z + 2 = 0\)

Cross-multiply plane normals \((1, 1, 1) \times (4, 1, -2)\) for DRs and set \(z = 0\) for a point:

Direction ratios \((l, m, n) = (1, 1, 1) \times (4, 1, -2)\)

\(= (-2 - 1, 4 - (-2), 1 - 4)\)

\(= (-3, 6, -3) \propto (1, -2, 1)\)

Putting \(z = 0\) in \(x + y\)

\(= -1\) and \(4x + y = -2\) gives:

\(x = -\frac{1}{3}, y = -\frac{2}{3}, z = 0\)

∴ Symmetric Form: \(\frac{x + \frac{1}{3}}{1} = \frac{y + \frac{2}{3}}{-2} = \frac{z}{1}\)

2. Coplanarity Verification & Plane Containing Two Lines

Evaluate the \(3\times 3\) coplanarity determinant:

Here \((x_{1}, y_{1}, z_{1}) = (-1, 3, -2), (x_{2}, y_{2}, z_{2}) = (0, 7, -7)\)

Determinant:

\(1 \cdot (4 - 3) - 4 \cdot (-6 - 1) - 5 \cdot (9 - 2) = 1 + 28 - 35 + 6\)

\(= 0\) (Coplanar)

Normal to the plane \(= (-3, 2, 1) \times (1, -3, 2)\)

\(= (7, 7, 7) \propto (1, 1, 1)\)

Plane through (−1, 3, −2):

\(1(x + 1) + 1(y - 3) + 1(z + 2) = 0\)

∴ \(x + y + z = 0\)

Syllabus Book Problems: Angle Between Line & Plane \((\sin \varphi )\) and Mirror Image of a Point in a Plane

★ 05. Worked Numerical Example · Syllabus Book · G. B. Thomas & R. L. Finney (Ch. 10) & M. R. Spiegel

  • The angle \(\varphi \) between a line (l, m, n) and a plane Ax + By + Cz \(+ D = 0\) is complementary to the angle with the plane normal: \(\sin \varphi = |\)Al + Bm + Cn\(\frac{|}{\sqrt{A^{2} + B^{2} + C^{2}}\sqrt{l^{2} + m^{2} + n^{2}}}\).
Angle \(\varphi \) Between a Line (l, m, n) and a Plane Ax + By + Cz \(+ D = 0\)
\(\sin \varphi = |\)Al + Bm + Cn\(\frac{|}{\sqrt{A^{2} + B^{2} + C^{2}} \cdot \sqrt{l^{2} + m^{2} + n^{2}}}\)
Problem / Theorem Question
  • (1) [Thomas & Finney] Find the angle between the line \(\frac{x - 1}{2} = \frac{y - 2}{1} = \frac{z - 3}{-2}\) and the plane \(x + 2y + 2z - 5 = 0\).
  • (2) Find the foot of the perpendicular and the mirror image of the point \(P(1, 3, 4)\) in the plane \(2x - y + z + 3 = 0\).
Step-by-step solution
1. Angle \(\varphi \) Between Line (2, 1, −2) and Plane \(x + 2y + 2z - 5 = 0\)

Apply \(\sin \varphi = |\)Al + Bm + Cn\(\frac{|}{\sqrt{A^{2}+B^{2}+C^{2}}\sqrt{l^{2}+m^{2}+n^{2}}}\):

\(\sin \varphi = |1(2) + 2(1) + \frac{2(-2)|}{\sqrt{1^{2} + 2^{2} + 2^{2}} \cdot \sqrt{2^{2} + 1^{2} + (-2)^{2}}}\)

\(= |2 + 2 - \frac{4|}{3 \times 3}\)

\(= 0\)

∴ \(\varphi = 0^{\circ}\) (The line is parallel to the plane)

2. Foot of Perpendicular \(N\) and Mirror Image P′ of \(P(1, 3, 4)\)

Write the normal line through \(P(1, 3, 4)\) with DRs (2, −1, 1):

Any point on the normal line is \(Q(2t + 1, -t + 3, t + 4)\).

For foot of perpendicular \(N\) on \(2x - y + z + 3 = 0\):

\(2(2t + 1) - (-t + 3) + (t + 4) + 3 = 0\)

\(\implies 6t + 6 = 0\)

\(\implies t = -1\)

\(\implies \) Foot \(N = (-1, 4, 3)\)

For mirror image \(P' (t = 2(-1) = -2)\):

∴ Foot \(N = (-1, 4, 3)\), Mirror Image \(P' = (-3, 5, 2)\)
